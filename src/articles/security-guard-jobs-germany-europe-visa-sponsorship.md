## Security Guard Jobs in Germany and Europe with Visa Sponsorship: A Practical Guide for 2026

Security guard roles can be sponsored in Germany, but the path is more structured than many candidates expect. Germany's recognised-skills immigration law — the Fachkräfte-Einwanderungsgesetz — opened a meaningful route for qualified tradespeople and vocationally trained workers, and security is explicitly included when applicants can demonstrate a recognised equivalent to German certification. This guide covers what the job involves, how sponsorship actually works, what qualifications you will need to obtain or convert, and how to protect yourself from the fraud that saturates this sector.

---

## 1. What the Work Actually Involves — and Why Germany Struggles to Fill It

Security guards in Germany perform a wider range of duties than the role sometimes implies elsewhere. Depending on the assignment, a security officer might be stationed at a critical infrastructure site such as a power facility or data centre, conduct access control and identity checks at a corporate campus, patrol public transport networks, provide airport security (a distinct and more heavily regulated sub-sector), protect retail premises, manage crowd safety at large-scale events, or work residential security for high-value properties.

The working pattern is demanding. Shifts of twelve hours are common, including nights, weekends, and public holidays. Lone working is frequent in some roles, while other assignments require close coordination with police, fire services, or building management. Incident reporting must be completed in German to a legally defensible standard. Guards working in aviation security face additional training requirements under EU regulation (EC) No 300/2008 and its implementing acts, which are enforced uniformly across EU member states.

Germany's security industry has reported persistent workforce shortfalls over recent years. Industry associations have publicly cited vacancies running into five figures nationally, driven by several converging pressures: growth in e-commerce warehousing requiring round-the-clock security, EU mandates on critical infrastructure protection, large infrastructure projects requiring construction site security, and a domestic working-age population that is not growing fast enough to fill roles. This structural shortage is real, but it does not automatically make visa approvals easier — the shortage creates the demand; the immigration rules govern the supply.

## 2. What It Pays

Salaries for security guards in Germany are quoted as annual gross figures or hourly gross rates. As of recent collective agreements, base pay for a §34a-certified guard under Tarifvertrag (collective wage agreement) has been increasing, but regional differences are significant — rates in Bavaria or Baden-Württemberg tend to be higher than in some eastern federal states, partly because collective bargaining coverage and cost of living differ.

**Indicative ranges (gross, full-time equivalent — verify with current tariff tables):**

| Role level | Indicative annual gross | Typical context |
|---|---|---|
| Basic security officer (§34a qualified) | €25,000–€30,000 | Retail, logistics, event security |
| Experienced / lead officer | €28,000–€35,000 | Corporate sites, public transport |
| Specialist (airport, critical infrastructure) | €32,000–€42,000+ | Licensed aviation or CI roles |
| Security supervisor / team leader | €35,000–€45,000 | Managing a site team |

These are indicative ranges and can vary materially based on the employer's size, whether they operate under a collective agreement, night-shift supplements (Nachtzuschläge), and the specific federal state. Night and holiday premiums can add meaningfully to take-home pay. Do not accept a figure from any source — including this guide — as a guarantee; always compare against current Tarifvertrag rates published by BDSW (Bundesverband der Sicherheitswirtschaft) or equivalent industry bodies.

## 3. The Visa Route in Detail

### The Skilled Worker Visa (Fachkräfte-Einwanderungsgesetz)

This is the primary route for most non-EU security professionals. It applies to people who hold a vocational qualification — not necessarily a university degree — that is formally recognised as equivalent to a German qualification. For security work, the relevant German benchmark is typically the Ausbildung in a security-related discipline (Fachkraft für Schutz und Sicherheit or Servicekraft für Schutz und Sicherheit), or demonstrated equivalence to the §34a Sachkundeprüfung.

**Who initiates the process:** The employer must make a concrete job offer and, in most cases, initiate the qualification recognition process on your behalf or require you to have completed it. The employer must also demonstrate that the role cannot be easily filled domestically — though this labour market test has been relaxed under recent reforms. Verify the current requirements on the official portal make-it-in-germany.com or with BAMF (Bundesamt für Migration und Flüchtlinge).

**What you must provide:**
- A signed job offer or employment contract from a German employer with a registered business address
- Evidence of your security qualification (original documents, certified translations into German)
- Proof of German language ability, typically at least B1 level for most security roles
- A valid passport and biometric photos meeting current requirements
- Proof of accommodation in Germany (your employer may help arrange this initially)
- Criminal record check from your home country and any country of prior residence
- Health insurance confirmation

**The visa is applied for at the German embassy or consulate in your home country.** You cannot apply from within Germany on a tourist visa. Processing times at consulates vary considerably; building at least three to four months of consulate processing time into your planning, on top of any recognition procedure, is prudent. Check the website of the specific German embassy handling your application for current appointment wait times — they differ widely by country.

### The Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte)

The Chancenkarte is a points-based route that allows entry to Germany to search for a job rather than arriving with one already confirmed. Points are awarded for factors including vocational qualifications, language ability, age, and previous stays in Germany. It does not grant full working rights immediately, but it can allow limited trial work while you search. For security, this might suit someone who wants to come to Germany, sit the §34a examination, and then negotiate a full employment contract from within the country. Be aware that the financial self-sufficiency requirements during the search period are real — you must be able to fund yourself. Verify current point thresholds and eligibility on make-it-in-germany.com.

### Why the EU Blue Card Is Not the Typical Route

The EU Blue Card requires a recognised university-level degree and a salary threshold currently set well above typical security guard salaries (thresholds are revised periodically; verify at bamf.de). While a small number of security management positions might approach relevance, a front-line security officer role will not meet these criteria in practice.

## 4. Qualifications, Licensing, and the Recognition Process

This is the most consequential section for most applicants, and it is where most people underestimate the timeline.

**The §34a GewO Sachkundeprüfung** is a statutory examination required for guards working in specific higher-risk settings: access control at venues with alcohol, protection of accommodation for asylum seekers, and any commercial security activity. It is administered by the Industrie- und Handelskammer (IHK) and covers German law related to security work, first aid, fire prevention, and professional conduct. The exam has a written and an oral component, both conducted in German.

**Formal qualification recognition (Anerkennung)** is a separate process. If you hold a national vocational qualification in security from your home country — for example, an SIA-linked qualification from the UK, a SAPS certificate from South Africa, or a national police background — you can apply to have it assessed for equivalency against German standards. The central recognition database is the ANABIN database and the official recognition portal is anerkennungsberatung.de. The competent body for security recognition is typically the relevant state authority (Ordnungsamt or Gewerbeamt at Landesebene, depending on the federal state).

Recognition outcomes have three possibilities: full equivalence, partial equivalence (requiring a compensatory measure such as an additional examination), or no equivalence. A partial recognition requiring a compensatory exam is common for many foreign security qualifications and is not a dead end — it simply adds time and study.

**Language documentation:** Most visa routes require a certificate at B1 minimum, typically from Goethe-Institut, telc, or ÖSD. For positions requiring significant public interaction or leadership, employers will often ask for B2. Arrange this before you apply — it is a common bottleneck.

## 5. Which Employers Actually Sponsor Security Workers

Not all German security companies recruit internationally, and those that do are typically larger operations with HR infrastructure capable of managing immigration paperwork.

**Companies likely to have sponsored or to sponsor international hires:**
- Large national security service groups (Securitas Deutschland, Dussmann Group, Wisag, KÖTTER Security, and similar) — these operate across multiple sectors and have formal recruitment pipelines
- Airport operators and their security subsidiaries, which operate under the aviation security regulatory framework and have structural staff needs
- Critical infrastructure operators (energy, water, telecommunications) who contract out to licensed security firms with known staffing pressures
- Exhibition and events companies with year-round staffing demands — Messe Frankfurt, Messe München, and similar venues

**How to identify genuine sponsors:**
- Check the employer's registration on the German Handelsregister (commercial register) at unternehmensregister.de
- Confirm they hold a valid security services licence (Bewachungsgewerbeerlaubnis) as required by §34a GewO
- Look for vacancies posted directly on their careers page, on the BA-Jobbörse (Federal Employment Agency job board), or on established platforms such as Indeed.de or Stepstone.de
- Ask directly in any application whether the employer can support a Skilled Worker visa application and what their process is

## 6. Mistakes That Get Applications Rejected — and How to Fix Them

1. **Submitting uncertified or untranslated documents.** German authorities require certified translations by a recognised translator. Fix: Use a sworn translator (vereidigter Übersetzer) listed with the relevant German court.

2. **Applying without an IHK-equivalent language certificate.** Employer assurances that language is fine are not accepted by consulates. Fix: Obtain a formal certificate before submitting your visa application.

3. **Starting the visa process before qualification recognition.** Some applicants apply to the consulate before the Anerkennung is confirmed, causing delays or refusals. Fix: Get at least a preliminary recognition decision before submission.

4. **Accepting a job offer from an employer who is not licensed under §34a GewO.** Working for an unlicensed company is illegal and will not support a valid visa application. Fix: Verify the employer's Bewachungsgewerbeerlaubnis before signing anything.

5. **Missing criminal record requirements.** Germany requires a Führungszeugnis (German police clearance) and an equivalent from your home country. Gaps in documentation cause refusals. Fix: Apply for both early; home-country certificates can take weeks.

6. **Underestimating the language requirement for the §34a exam.** People assume a conversational level is sufficient. The exam uses legal and technical German. Fix: Prepare specifically for the exam vocabulary and legal terminology, not just everyday German.

7. **Applying through a third party who promises to secure the visa.** No agent can apply for or guarantee a German work visa. Fix: Work only with employers who involve you directly in the official process.

8. **Failing to secure health insurance confirmation before the visa appointment.** Germany requires proof of health insurance coverage from arrival. Fix: Arrange a statutory health insurance (gesetzliche Krankenversicherung) registration or a recognised private policy before your consulate appointment.

## 7. Realistic Timeline from Application to Arrival

Timelines depend heavily on your home country, the efficiency of the specific German state authority handling recognition, and consulate appointment availability. The following is a realistic planning estimate, not a guarantee.

| Phase | Typical duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Language certification | 2–4 months | Including preparation and exam sitting |
| Qualification recognition (Anerkennung) | 3–6 months | Longer if compensatory measures required |
| Finding a qualifying employer | 1–6 months (concurrent) | Can overlap with recognition process |
| Employer issues job offer and supports visa file | 2–4 weeks | Depends on employer HR capacity |
| Consulate appointment and visa processing | 2–4 months | Varies significantly by country and consulate |
| **Realistic total (from decision to move)** | **6–14 months** | Plan for the longer end |

Trying to compress this timeline by skipping steps — particularly recognition or language certification — consistently backfires. Employers with experience hiring internationally will often advise you to start the language and recognition processes before you even have a job offer, precisely because the waiting is the longest part.

## Scam Warning: How to Protect Yourself

Security guard roles in Europe are heavily targeted by job and visa scams, and the pattern is consistent: a convincing-looking company emails or messages you about an urgent vacancy, offers an attractive salary, and then asks for a fee to process your work permit, arrange your travel, or reserve your position.

**No legitimate employer or visa process will ever ask you to pay for a job offer, a work permit, a visa application, or a security deposit.** German visas are applied for at a German embassy or consulate in your home country, using official channels, and fees are fixed and low. Nobody can buy your visa or bypass the process for a fee.

**How to verify a potential employer:**
- Look them up on the German commercial register at unternehmensregister.de — every legitimately trading German company is listed
- Search for their Bewachungsgewerbeerlaubnis — ask them to provide the licence number and verify it with the relevant Gewerbeamt
- Verify that any recruiter is genuine by checking their registration and whether the job appears on the employer's own website
- Call the employer's switchboard number (found independently, not from the email they sent you) and ask whether the vacancy exists

If an offer arrives unsolicited with pressure to respond quickly, asks for personal or bank details early in the process, or requests any form of payment, stop contact immediately. Report scams to the Bundesnetzagentur (Federal Network Agency) or to Action Fraud in the UK if you are UK-based.
