*Checked 11 November 2026 against USCIS and the EB-5 Reform and Integrity Act of 2022.*

There are four serious routes into the United States for someone whose claim rests on a business. They differ structurally, not just in price, and the one with the biggest advertising budget is not the one most people should take.

| Route | Capital required | Status | Tied to |
|---|---|---|---|
| EB-5 | $800,000 or $1,050,000 | Permanent residence | The investment |
| E-2 | No published minimum | Temporary, renewable | The enterprise |
| L-1 | None | Temporary, renewable | Your existing company |
| O-1A | None | Temporary, renewable | Your own record |

## EB-5: the figures

The EB-5 Reform and Integrity Act of 2022 set the minimum investment at:

- **$1,050,000** standard
- **$800,000** in a Targeted Employment Area

A Targeted Employment Area is a rural area or one with high unemployment, and most EB-5 investment goes into TEA projects.

You must create **at least 10 full-time positions** for qualifying employees, full-time meaning a minimum of **35 working hours per week**. Outside a regional centre the business must create those jobs directly on its own payroll. Inside a regional centre, indirect and induced jobs calculated by an economic model can count — the main reason the regional centre model dominates.

### Indexation on 1 January 2027

From that date, and every five years after, the standard minimum is adjusted for inflation using the Consumer Price Index, with TEA and infrastructure minimums set at **60 per cent of the standard**.

That is weeks away at the time of writing. If the current figure sits at the edge of what you can raise, the timing of your petition is a financial question.

### The official page contradicts itself

USCIS's "About the EB-5 Visa Classification" page is, as at this check, internally inconsistent. Its capital table still shows the **pre-2022 amounts** — $1,800,000 and $900,000 for petitions filed on or after 21 November 2019, and $1,000,000 and $500,000 before. A separate alert on the same page refers to $1 million and $500,000 following a court ruling. And the section carries a note saying it is being updated to reflect the 2022 Act.

Take your figures from the first table you find on a government site and you will plan around a number that has not been operative for years. Verify with USCIS or an immigration attorney before moving money.

The Regional Center Program is authorised through **30 September 2027**.

## What EB-5 involves

1. Choose a project, or structure your own business.
2. Invest, or irrevocably commit the funds.
3. File the immigrant petition, documenting the lawful source of every dollar.
4. Adjust status or go through consular processing.
5. Receive conditional permanent residence, valid two years.
6. File to remove conditions, proving the investment was sustained and jobs created.
7. Receive unconditional permanent residence.

**Source of funds is harder than raising the money.** Not the amount — the provenance. Every dollar needs a paper trail satisfying an officer who has never met you. Applicants from countries with informal banking records, or whose wealth accumulated in cash-intensive businesses, routinely find this the hardest part.

**The money is genuinely at risk, and must be.** A guaranteed return would defeat the statutory purpose and the petition would fail. Projects do fail. A failure can cost you the capital and the immigration outcome together.

## Regional centre or direct

**Regional centre.** You invest into a pooled project, usually real estate or infrastructure. Indirect and induced jobs can count toward your ten, so you need not create ten payroll positions. You are a limited partner and manage nothing. But you control nothing, the outcome depends on someone else's execution, project quality varies enormously, and authorisation runs only to 30 September 2027 — a programme that has lapsed before.

**Direct investment.** You invest in your own business and keep control. But you must create ten direct full-time positions and sustain them. On $800,000, ten real jobs is demanding and many honest business plans cannot support it.

If you want to operate a business, the E-2 is usually the better instrument unless permanent residence is non-negotiable.

### Due diligence on a project

- Read the offering documents in full, risk factors included.
- Understand the capital stack. EB-5 capital is frequently the most junior money in a deal, which is why it is available at the price the developer is paying.
- Check the job creation methodology — a credentialed economist, an accepted model, and a cushion above the bare minimum.
- Ask about the developer's track record on previous EB-5 projects specifically.
- Ask what repays you, on what timeline, and what happens if a refinance is unavailable.
- Treat a guaranteed return as a red flag. It threatens the at-risk requirement.

## Removing conditions

Conditional residence lasts two years. Before it expires you must petition to remove conditions, showing the capital remained invested and at risk and that the jobs were created.

If the project underperformed, your petition can fail years after you invested, with your family's status attached.

Keep your own records of every transfer and project report rather than relying on the regional centre two years later. And note that a project returning capital *early* is also a problem, because it must remain at risk for the required period.

## E-2: no minimum

Open to nationals of countries with a qualifying treaty with the United States. If your country is not on the State Department's list, this route does not exist for you.

There is **no statutory minimum**. The investment must be "substantial" relative to the total cost of purchasing or establishing the enterprise, and the business must be real, active and operating — not passive property or securities, and not marginal, meaning it must do more than provide a living for you and your family.

A small business can qualify. People have obtained E-2 visas on investments well below $100,000 where the business genuinely operated. No figure is published because the test is proportional.

**What you get.** Temporary and renewable, potentially indefinitely, as long as the business keeps qualifying. No green card on its own. Your spouse can generally obtain work authorisation. Children lose derivative status at 21.

**Renewal is not automatic.** The business must still qualify at each renewal. Businesses that grow, hire and file proper accounts renew easily. Businesses that stagnate are exactly what the marginality test is designed to catch.

## The two routes people miss

**The L-1.** If you already own or work for a business outside the United States and it establishes or owns a US entity, the L-1 moves you across as an executive, manager or specialised-knowledge employee. You generally need twelve months of qualifying employment with the foreign company in the preceding three years.

For an established business owner this is frequently better than both E-2 and EB-5. No capital threshold, and the L-1A for executives and managers has a well-worn path to a green card through EB-1C, which sidesteps the per-country backlogs dominating other employment-based categories.

It is missed because people search for "investor visa" and the L-1 is not marketed as one.

**The O-1A.** For individuals with extraordinary ability in business, science, education or athletics. Evidence-based, no investment, no cap. Founders with a documented record — funding raised, press coverage, awards, companies built — qualify more often than they expect.

## Choosing

**EB-5** if you want permanent residence, have the capital, can document its source, and accept genuine commercial risk.

**E-2** if your country has a treaty, you want to run the business, and can live without permanent status.

**L-1** if you already own or work for a business outside the US with a US entity. Cheapest and fastest for most established business owners.

**O-1A** if you have a documented record and would rather deploy evidence than capital.

**None of them** if the real objective is a work visa and the business is a device. The marginality test exists precisely to catch that.

## Why country of chargeability dominates EB-5 timing

Employment-based immigrant visas are subject to per-country limits. If you are chargeable to a country with heavy EB-5 demand, the wait for an available visa number can be the largest component of your timeline, measured in years, and no amount of expedited petition processing changes it.

Spouses can sometimes be charged to a different country, which occasionally transforms a timeline. Check before anything else.

The E-2, L-1 and O-1A involve no immigrant visa number and are not subject to the backlogs at all — one of the under-appreciated advantages of the temporary routes.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much do I need for EB-5?**
$800,000 in a Targeted Employment Area or $1,050,000 otherwise, indexed from 1 January 2027.

**Is there a minimum for E-2?**
No published minimum. Substantial relative to the cost of the business.

**Does EB-5 give a green card immediately?**
No. Conditional residence for two years, then unconditional.

**Can I get the EB-5 money back?**
Potentially, after conditions are removed and depending on project terms. It cannot be guaranteed.

**Does the money have to be my own?**
It must be lawfully obtained and yours to invest. Gifted and borrowed funds can qualify, subject to rules about security and the lender's source.

**Can I move from E-2 to a green card?**
Not directly. Typically through a separate category, or EB-1C if the business grows.

**Does buying property count?**
No. Passive real estate is not an active commercial enterprise.

**Can my spouse work?**
On the E-2, generally yes with work authorisation. EB-5 conditional residents have full work authorisation.

## Costs beyond the investment

- **Legal fees.** Substantial on all routes. On EB-5 the attorney work is a drafting exercise as much as a collection one, and it is not a formality.
- **Project administrative fees.** Regional centre deals charge a fee on top of the investment, frequently in the tens of thousands.
- **Filing fees** for the petitions and for each family member's status application.
- **Accounting and business valuation**, especially on the E-2 where proportionality must be demonstrated.
- **Translation and document procurement.** On an EB-5 source-of-funds file spanning several countries and decades, this adds up substantially.
- **Ongoing compliance.** An operating business on an E-2 has US tax filings, payroll obligations and state registrations from day one.

## What a source-of-funds file actually contains

This is the part that defeats most EB-5 applicants, and it is worth seeing the shape of it before deciding the route is for you.

You are proving a chain: where the money is now, how it got there, and how it was lawfully earned at every step. For an investor whose wealth came from a business built over twenty years, that can mean company formation documents, two decades of audited accounts, tax returns for every year, dividend resolutions, bank statements tracing each transfer, property sale contracts, and certified translations of all of it.

Where funds are gifted, the donor's own source of funds must be documented to the same standard. Where funds are borrowed, the loan agreement, the security, and the lender's source all come into scope.

Officers are looking for gaps. A transfer with no explanation, a year with no tax return, a sum that appears without a source — any of these generates a request for evidence and can sink the petition.

Begin assembling this before you choose a project. People routinely commit to a deal and then discover they cannot document the money.

## Timing, and why country of chargeability dominates

Employment-based immigrant visas are subject to per-country limits. If you are chargeable to a country with heavy EB-5 demand, the wait for an available visa number can be the single largest component of your timeline — measured in years — and no amount of expedited petition processing changes it.

Spouses can sometimes be charged to a different country, which occasionally transforms a timeline entirely. Check this before anything else.

The E-2, L-1 and O-1A involve no immigrant visa number and are not subject to these backlogs at all. For someone from a heavily oversubscribed country, that difference can outweigh every other consideration in this article.

## What nobody can honestly tell you

There is no current, meaningful published success rate for any of these routes. Approval statistics lag and are not broken down in ways that tell you anything about your own case. Processing times move. And the variables that matter most — documentation quality, project viability, officer discretion — appear in no published figure.

Anyone quoting you a confident timeline or a success rate is guessing or selling.

## The E-2 renewal question

An E-2 is granted for a period and renewed, and renewal is not automatic. The business must still qualify at each renewal — still real, still operating, still not marginal, with the investment still substantial relative to the enterprise.

In practice this means the business has to actually work. A company that limps along generating just enough to support the family is at risk at renewal precisely because the marginality test is designed to catch it. Businesses that grow, hire and file proper accounts renew without difficulty.

Plan for this at the outset. The business plan you file is not only a document for the first application; it is the standard against which you will be measured later.
