**Veterans and eligible surviving spouses can purchase a home with no down payment, no private mortgage insurance, and competitive interest rates through the US Department of Veterans Affairs home loan guarantee programme. Understanding who qualifies, what the loan actually costs in fees and interest, and how the application process works is essential before you approach a lender — and this guide covers all three in detail.**

---

## What Is a VA Home Loan?

A VA home loan is not a loan made directly by the government. It is a mortgage issued by a private lender — a bank, credit union, or mortgage company — that is partially guaranteed by the Department of Veterans Affairs. That government guarantee is what allows lenders to offer favourable terms: no requirement for a down payment on most purchases, no private mortgage insurance (PMI), and interest rates that have historically been competitive with or below conventional equivalents.

The programme was established under the Servicemen's Readjustment Act of 1944 (commonly called the GI Bill) and has since helped millions of veterans and service members purchase, build, or refinance homes. It is administered by the VA's Loan Guaranty Service. The rules, fee tables, and property requirements are maintained in the VA Lender Handbook (also called Circular 26 guidance), which lenders must follow and which is publicly available at VA.gov.

---

## Who Qualifies? VA Loan Eligibility Explained

### Service requirements: the baseline threshold

Eligibility is tied to your military service history, and the minimum service requirements vary depending on when you served and your service component. Broadly, the qualifying groups are:

- **Active-duty service members** who have served a minimum continuous period (generally 90 days of active duty during wartime or 181 days during peacetime, though thresholds shift by era — always verify at VA.gov).
- **Veterans** discharged under conditions other than dishonorable, who met the service minimums applicable to their period of service.
- **National Guard and Reserve members** who have completed six years of service in the Selected Reserve or National Guard, or who were called to active duty under specific federal orders and served the required period.
- **Surviving spouses** of veterans who died in service or as a result of a service-connected disability, provided the spouse has not remarried (with limited exceptions for spouses who remarry after age 57 — confirm current rules at VA.gov).

The nature of your discharge matters. A dishonorable discharge generally disqualifies you. A general discharge or discharge under honorable conditions typically does not. If your discharge status is in question, you can apply for a Character of Discharge determination from the VA.

### Obtaining your Certificate of Eligibility

Before any lender can proceed with a VA loan, they need your Certificate of Eligibility (COE), which is the document the VA issues to confirm you meet service requirements. There are three ways to obtain it:

1. **Online via eBenefits** at ebenefits.va.gov — the fastest route for most veterans.
2. **Through your lender** — most VA-approved lenders can retrieve your COE electronically through the VA's Automated Certificate of Eligibility (ACE) system in minutes.
3. **By post** using VA Form 26-1880, sent to the VA Eligibility Center in Winston-Salem, North Carolina — allow several weeks for processing.

Your COE will state your entitlement amount. Veterans who have never used a VA loan, or who have fully restored their entitlement, typically have "full entitlement," which means no county loan limit applies from the VA's side (individual lender policies may still apply).

### Occupancy requirements

VA loans are for primary residences only. You must certify that you intend to personally occupy the property as your home within a reasonable time after closing — generally 60 days, with extensions available in limited circumstances (for example, if a spouse occupies the home while the veteran is deployed). Vacation homes and pure investment properties do not qualify.

## VA Loan Benefits: What Makes It Different

### No down payment

For most borrowers using full entitlement, a VA loan requires no down payment. On a $400,000 purchase, that means you can buy without putting up the $14,000–$80,000 that a conventional or FHA loan might require. This is one of the largest financial advantages of the programme, particularly for veterans early in their civilian careers who have not yet accumulated significant savings.

### No private mortgage insurance

Conventional loans with less than 20% down typically require PMI, which can add $100–$300 per month (or more) to the payment on a mid-sized loan. VA loans have no PMI equivalent. The VA Funding Fee (covered below) serves a similar programme-sustainability purpose but is a one-time cost, not a recurring monthly charge.

### Competitive interest rates

Because the VA guarantees a portion of each loan, lenders take on less default risk, which tends to translate into interest rates that are at or below conventional market rates. The actual rate you receive depends on your credit profile, the lender, and prevailing market conditions — shop at least three to five VA-approved lenders and compare Loan Estimates (the standardised three-page document lenders must provide within three business days of application under RESPA).

### Limits on closing costs

The VA restricts which fees lenders can charge veterans. Lenders may charge a 1% origination fee in lieu of itemised fees, or they may itemise fees up to that equivalent. Certain charges — such as attorney fees not required by the state, escrow fees above actual cost, and settlement or closing fees beyond a set threshold — are not allowable under VA rules. Review VA Pamphlet 26-7 (the VA Lender Handbook) or ask your lender for a breakdown against the VA's allowable/non-allowable fee list.

### Assumability

VA loans are assumable, meaning a future buyer can take over your loan under its existing interest rate, subject to lender and VA approval. In a rising rate environment, a low-rate VA loan could be a significant selling point. Note that if a non-veteran assumes the loan, your entitlement tied to that loan is not restored until the loan is paid off — unless the assumptor is also a veteran and agrees to substitute their entitlement.

### Foreclosure avoidance assistance

The VA operates a loan technician programme and works with servicers on behalf of veterans in financial difficulty. This is not a guarantee against foreclosure, but the VA has a financial interest in helping borrowers avoid it, which means its servicing oversight adds a layer of protection that conventional loans do not have.

## What Does a VA Loan Cost?

### The VA Funding Fee

The VA Funding Fee is the programme's primary cost and is charged to help fund future veterans' benefits without placing the burden on taxpayers. It is calculated as a percentage of the loan amount and can be financed into the loan rather than paid at closing.

The fee percentage depends on:

- Whether it is your first or subsequent use of the VA loan benefit.
- Your service component (regular military vs. Reserve/National Guard).
- The size of your down payment (putting down 5% or 10% reduces the fee).

**Illustrative fee ranges (verify current tables at VA.gov before proceeding):**

| Use | Down Payment | Illustrative Fee (Regular Military) |
|-----|-------------|-------------------------------------|
| First use | 0% | ~2.15% |
| First use | 5%–9.99% | ~1.50% |
| First use | 10% or more | ~1.25% |
| Subsequent use | 0% | ~3.30% |
| Subsequent use | 5%–9.99% | ~1.50% |
| Subsequent use | 10% or more | ~1.25% |

*These figures are illustrative. Fee tables change and are published on the VA's website. Always verify the current fee schedule at VA.gov before committing to a loan.*

**Who is exempt from the Funding Fee?**

Veterans receiving VA disability compensation (at any rating percentage), surviving spouses of veterans who died in service or from a service-connected disability, and active-duty service members who have received a Purple Heart are generally exempt. Your lender will request documentation of your disability rating from the VA; confirm your exempt status before closing, because refunds after the fact are possible but involve an administrative process.

### Interest rate considerations

VA loans do not come with a government-set interest rate — your rate is negotiated with the lender. Rates fluctuate daily with the broader bond market. Use the Loan Estimate form to compare the Annual Percentage Rate (APR) across lenders, which incorporates fees as well as the interest rate and gives a more accurate picture of total cost.

### Illustrative worked example

*The following figures are for illustration only and do not represent current market conditions or guaranteed loan terms.*

Suppose a veteran purchases a home for $380,000 using a VA loan with full entitlement and no down payment. It is their first use of the benefit, and they are not exempt from the Funding Fee.

- Loan amount before fee: $380,000
- VA Funding Fee (~2.15%): approximately $8,170
- Total loan amount if fee is financed: approximately $388,170
- At an illustrative 6.5% interest rate on a 30-year term, the principal and interest payment would be approximately $2,454/month
- No PMI added
- No down payment required from savings

A comparable conventional loan at the same rate with 5% down ($19,000) on $380,000 would carry PMI of perhaps $150–$200/month until the loan-to-value ratio dropped below 80%. The VA borrower avoids that recurring cost, though they carry a slightly larger loan balance due to the financed Funding Fee.

This example demonstrates the trade-off, not a prediction. Your actual figures will depend on your credit profile, the lender, property taxes, homeowner's insurance, and market rates at the time of purchase.

## Types of VA Loans

### VA Purchase Loan

The most common type — used to buy a primary residence. Can be used for single-family homes, condominiums on the VA's approved list, manufactured homes meeting VA requirements, and properties with up to four units (provided the veteran occupies one).

### VA Interest Rate Reduction Refinance Loan (IRRRL)

Also called the "VA Streamline Refinance," the IRRRL allows veterans with an existing VA loan to refinance into a lower interest rate with minimal documentation and no new appraisal required in most cases. The property does not need to be your current primary residence (though it must have been at origination). The Funding Fee for an IRRRL is lower — check VA.gov for the current rate.

### VA Cash-Out Refinance

Allows veterans to refinance any existing mortgage (not just a VA loan) and take cash out of their equity, up to 100% of appraised value in some cases. The full Funding Fee applies on cash-out refinances unless the veteran is exempt. A new appraisal is required.

### VA Native American Direct Loan (NADL)

For Native American veterans who want to purchase, build, or improve a home on federal trust land. The VA acts as the direct lender rather than guaranteeing a private lender's loan. This programme has specific eligibility requirements beyond standard VA eligibility.

## The VA Loan Process: Step by Step

### Step 1: Obtain your Certificate of Eligibility

As described above — through eBenefits, your lender, or VA Form 26-1880.

### Step 2: Find a VA-approved lender

Not every mortgage lender participates in the VA programme. Search the VA's lender locator or ask lenders directly whether they are VA-approved. Banks, credit unions, mortgage brokers, and online lenders all operate in this space. Get pre-approval from at least two or three lenders before house hunting.

### Step 3: Find a property and make an offer

Work with a real estate agent familiar with VA transactions — they will understand the VA appraisal process and can draft your offer with appropriate contingencies. Include a VA escape clause (VA Amendatory Clause / Notice of Value contingency), which protects you if the property appraises below the purchase price.

### Step 4: VA appraisal and underwriting

Your lender orders the VA appraisal through the VA's system — you cannot choose the appraiser. The VA-assigned appraiser assesses both market value and compliance with Minimum Property Requirements (MPRs). If the appraiser identifies issues — a leaking roof, exposed wiring, inadequate water supply — these must generally be resolved before closing. Underwriting then reviews your financial documents (W-2s, pay stubs, bank statements, tax returns) alongside the COE and appraisal.

### Step 5: Closing

At closing, you sign the loan documents, pay any allowable closing costs not rolled into the loan, and the lender funds the purchase. If you are financing the Funding Fee, it appears on your Closing Disclosure as part of the loan amount.

## Common Mistakes VA Borrowers Make (and How to Fix Them)

1. **Not shopping multiple lenders.** The VA does not set your interest rate. A half-percentage-point difference on a $350,000 loan costs or saves tens of thousands over 30 years. Fix: collect Loan Estimates from at least three VA-approved lenders and compare APRs, not just headline rates.

2. **Assuming the VA appraisal is a home inspection.** The VA appraisal checks value and MPRs — it is not a detailed structural or systems inspection. Fix: always commission an independent home inspection from a licensed inspector before waiving contingencies.

3. **Forgetting to confirm Funding Fee exemption status.** Veterans with a pending disability claim sometimes close before the rating is finalised and pay a fee they were entitled to have waived. Fix: if a disability claim is in process, discuss timing with your lender — a refund is available if the rating is granted retroactively, but it requires filing a claim with the VA.

4. **Using entitlement on a property they keep as a rental.** If you buy with a VA loan and later convert the property to a rental without selling it or paying off the loan, your entitlement tied to that loan remains in use. Buying again with VA financing requires either restoring entitlement (by selling and paying off the first loan) or having sufficient remaining bonus entitlement. Fix: plan your housing strategy with a VA-knowledgeable lender before your second purchase.

5. **Not checking condo approval status.** A condominium complex must be on the VA's approved condo list for a VA loan to be used. Many perfectly good condos are not on the list. Fix: before making an offer on a condo, search the VA's approved condo database or ask the HOA if they have pursued VA approval.

6. **Letting the seller pay all closing costs without checking VA rules.** Sellers can pay all of the buyer's closing costs and up to 4% of the loan value in concessions under VA rules. This can reduce your out-of-pocket significantly. Fix: instruct your agent to negotiate seller concessions explicitly — many first-time VA borrowers leave this on the table.

## Special Situations

### Surviving spouses

Surviving spouses of veterans who died in service or from a service-connected disability may be eligible for VA loan benefits in their own right. The surviving spouse must not have remarried (with limited exceptions as noted). Eligibility is confirmed through the COE process, and a surviving spouse who was co-borrower on a VA loan with the deceased veteran may have additional options — verify at VA.gov or with a VA-approved lender.

### Veterans with partial entitlement

If you used a VA loan previously and still have an outstanding balance on it, you may have "remaining entitlement" that can be used for a second purchase, though lenders may require a down payment to make up the difference between the remaining guarantee and the county conforming loan limit. A VA-approved lender can calculate your remaining entitlement from your COE.

### Joint loans with non-veteran co-borrowers

You can take out a VA loan with a non-veteran co-borrower (for example, a civilian spouse). The VA guarantee only covers the veteran's portion of the loan, so lenders may require a down payment equal to 25% of the non-veteran's share. Speak with your lender about how this affects the loan structure.

### Active-duty service members

Active-duty members can use the VA loan benefit while still serving, provided they meet the minimum service threshold. Deployment can affect the occupancy requirement — a spouse occupying the home satisfies the VA's occupancy rule in most cases.

## Where to Get Help

The VA's official resource for the home loan programme is **VA.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans**, where you can find the current COE application portal, the approved condo list, the Funding Fee tables, and the VA Lender Handbook. The VA also operates regional Loan Guaranty offices that can answer programme questions.

For personalised guidance on your specific financial situation — including whether a VA loan is the right product given your credit profile, income, and long-term plans — work with a HUD-approved housing counsellor (free or low-cost, findable at consumerfinance.gov/find-a-housing-counselor) or a licensed mortgage professional with demonstrated VA loan experience.

## Key Takeaways

The VA home loan is a genuinely powerful benefit — zero down payment, no ongoing mortgage insurance, restricted closing costs, and a competitive rate environment — but it is not unconditional. Your eligibility depends on verified service history confirmed through the COE process. The loan must be used for a primary residence. The VA Funding Fee is a real cost, though it is waived for veterans with disability ratings. The VA appraisal is more demanding than a conventional one.

Used well, the VA loan can allow a veteran to purchase a home years earlier than they could through conventional financing, and with a significantly lower monthly payment than an equivalent FHA or PMI-carrying conventional loan. Understanding the mechanics in advance — service thresholds, entitlement, the Funding Fee, allowable costs, and the appraisal process — puts you in the strongest position when you walk into a lender's office or open an online application.
