**For Employers — and for any candidate or HR professional who needs to verify a hiring organisation's status.**

**You can check whether a UK employer holds a valid sponsor licence in under two minutes using the publicly available Register of Licensed Sponsors on GOV.UK. The register is maintained by UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI), is updated daily, and is the single authoritative source for this information.** What it tells you, what it does not tell you, and what to do when a search returns no result — that is what this guide covers in full.

---

## Why Verifying a Sponsor Licence Matters

For an HR manager or business owner, this question usually arises in one of three situations: you are acquiring a business and need to know whether the target company can legally employ its overseas staff; you are a candidate offered a job and want to confirm the employer is genuine; or you are an employer yourself and want to understand how your own entry on the register appears to candidates and other stakeholders.

Each situation carries real stakes. An employee who accepts a job on the strength of a promised sponsorship, only to discover the employer holds no licence, faces serious consequences — a refusal of their visa application, potential loss of their current immigration status, and wasted application costs. For the employer, making representations about sponsorship capability that turn out to be false can draw regulatory and reputational consequences.

It is also worth noting plainly: no legitimate employer or agent should ever charge a worker for the cost of the sponsor licence, the Certificate of Sponsorship, or any other immigration fee associated with bringing that worker to the UK. If payment is being demanded from the candidate side, treat it as a serious warning sign.

## Where to Find the Official Register of Licensed Sponsors

### The GOV.UK Register

Navigate to the official UKVI page titled "Register of licensed sponsors: workers" (for employer-sponsored work routes) or "Register of licensed sponsors: students" (for education providers). For employment purposes, you want the workers register.

The direct URL sits within the GOV.UK immigration guidance pages. Search for "register of licensed sponsors" on GOV.UK to find the current link, as URL structures on government sites can change. The register is a downloadable file — typically a spreadsheet — not a live search field. Download the most recent version, which is timestamped, and search within it.

### What the Register Contains

Each row in the register represents one licensed organisation. The columns you will use most often are:

| Column | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Organisation Name | The legal name of the licence holder |
| Town/City | Registered location of the organisation |
| County | County or region |
| Type | Worker, Temporary Worker, or both |
| Route | The specific sub-routes covered (e.g. Skilled Worker, Health and Care Worker, Seasonal Worker) |

There is no column for the number of workers currently sponsored, the number of Certificates of Sponsorship (CoS) remaining in the allocation, or any detail about the employer's compliance history. Those are visible only to the sponsor themselves via the Sponsor Management System (SMS), and to UKVI.

### What the Register Does Not Show

The register lists sponsors whose licences are currently active. It does not list sponsors whose licences have been suspended (suspended sponsors are removed from the published register until reinstatement) or revoked (permanently removed). A company that does not appear may have had its licence removed — or may simply never have held one. The register therefore tells you "this employer is currently authorised to sponsor" but cannot by itself confirm a full compliance history.

## Step-by-Step: How to Run the Check

### Step 1 — Download the Current File

Go to GOV.UK and locate the Register of Licensed Sponsors: Workers. Download the file. Check the date in the filename or header row; UKVI updates it daily on working days. If you are making a time-sensitive decision, note the date so you can confirm you are using the latest version.

### Step 2 — Search by Organisation Name

Open the file in a spreadsheet application. Use Ctrl+F (or your application's Find function) to search for the employer's legal registered name. Try partial strings if the full name returns nothing — a company called "Meridian Healthcare Solutions Limited" might be indexed differently from "Meridian Healthcare."

If you do not know the legal name, check the employer's Companies House entry. You can search Companies House at find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk using the trading name or company number.

### Step 3 — Check the Type and Route Columns

Finding the company name is not enough. Confirm that the "Type" column shows "Worker" (not only "Temporary Worker") if the role being offered is on the Skilled Worker route. Then check the "Route" column to confirm the specific sub-route relevant to your situation — for example, a care employer sponsoring overseas nurses must hold a licence with the Health and Care Worker route active.

Mismatches between the licence type and the offered role are a source of real confusion. An employer may hold a Temporary Worker licence for Seasonal Worker roles but have no Worker licence for Skilled Worker applications. These are separate licences with separate applications and separate obligations.

### Step 4 — If the Company Does Not Appear

Do not assume fraud immediately. Consider:

- The company may trade under a name different from its registered legal name — try both.
- The company may have applied for a licence that has not yet been granted. UKVI processing times vary; see [UK Sponsor Licence Application Timeline: How Long It Takes](/how-long-sponsor-licence-application-takes/) for a realistic picture of current processing windows.
- The company may have a very recently granted licence that has not yet propagated to the published version of the register.
- The company genuinely has no licence.

Ask the employer directly to confirm their licence reference and provide a screenshot from their Sponsor Management System dashboard showing their current licence status. A legitimate employer will not object to this request.

## Reading the Result: What Different Statuses Mean in Practice

### Active Licence — What This Confirms and What It Does Not

If you find the company on the register with the correct type and route, you have confirmed that the Home Office has granted the licence and it is currently active. This means the employer is authorised to issue Certificates of Sponsorship to overseas workers on that route.

It does not mean the employer is compliant with all ongoing duties. Sponsor licence holders carry substantial continuing obligations — record-keeping, right to work checks, reporting employee changes to UKVI within specified timeframes, and maintaining an adequate HR system. These compliance duties continue throughout the licence period. The register does not grade or rate compliance. For a full picture of what those duties involve, see [Sponsor Licence Compliance Duties After Approval 2026](/sponsor-licence-compliance-duties-after-approval/).

### No Entry Found

As discussed above, absence from the register means either the licence does not exist, has been revoked, or is suspended. It can also mean a very recent grant that has not yet been published. Do not proceed on the assumption that the licence exists until you see evidence.

If you are an employer who has recently had a licence granted and your entry has not appeared within two to three working days, contact UKVI via the sponsor helpline to confirm and report the issue.

### A Note on Suspended Licences

Suspended sponsors are removed from the active register. If you had previously found a company on the register and now cannot, suspension is one possibility. You can check recent Home Office enforcement actions in press releases on GOV.UK, though these cover only more significant cases. For day-to-day suspensions, the register itself — and its absence — is your main signal.

## Common Mistakes When Checking Sponsor Licence Status

The following errors appear regularly and have concrete consequences.

1. **Checking once and assuming permanence.** A licence that exists today may be suspended or revoked tomorrow following a UKVI compliance visit or an investigation triggered by a report. If you are entering a long-term employment arrangement that depends on sponsorship, periodic re-checks — at least before any major immigration milestone such as a CoS issue or visa renewal — are good practice.

   *Fix:* Set a calendar reminder to re-download and re-check the register every six months, and always check immediately before you issue or accept a Certificate of Sponsorship.

2. **Conflating trading name with legal name.** A recruiter may tell a candidate that "Bright Futures Care Group" is a licensed sponsor, but the legal entity may be "Bright Futures Healthcare Limited." Searching only for the trading name returns nothing, which some candidates incorrectly interpret as the company having no licence.

   *Fix:* Always verify the registered legal name at Companies House before searching the register.

3. **Assuming the right route is covered.** An employer in the care sector may have a Worker licence, but if the Health and Care Worker sub-route is not listed, they cannot use the lower application fee and faster processing that route offers — and in some cases cannot sponsor at all for roles that require that specific route.

   *Fix:* Check the "Route" column explicitly. Do not assume that any Worker licence covers all Skilled Worker sub-routes.

4. **Relying on a cached or printed version of the register.** The file is updated daily. Using a file from last week or last month may show a company as active when its licence has since been suspended.

   *Fix:* Always download a fresh copy on the day you are making a decision.

5. **Not asking the employer for supplementary confirmation.** The register shows status but no reference number or contact. An employer unable or unwilling to show their SMS dashboard to an authorised colleague, or to provide written confirmation of their licence reference number, warrants further scrutiny.

   *Fix:* Request the organisation's SMS reference or ask your HR counterpart to generate a confirmation letter from within the SMS.

6. **Ignoring the Type/Route mismatch.** A candidate offered a Skilled Worker role at a company that holds only a Temporary Worker licence cannot be legally sponsored for that role, regardless of what the employer says.

   *Fix:* Match the route on the register to the Immigration Rules category applicable to the offered role. If you are unsure which category applies, consult a licensed immigration solicitor or OISC-regulated adviser before proceeding.

## Illustrative Example: How This Works in Practice

The following is illustrative only and does not represent a real company or real individuals.

**Scenario:** A UK-based social care provider, "Sunnymead Care Limited," wants to hire a senior care assistant from Nigeria. The HR manager has been told by a recruitment agency that Sunnymead holds a sponsor licence. Before issuing a job offer and incurring costs, the HR manager downloads the Register of Licensed Sponsors: Workers from GOV.UK.

She searches for "Sunnymead Care Limited" — no result. She then searches Companies House, where she finds the company is registered as "Sunnymead Healthcare & Support Services Limited." Searching that name in the register returns a match. The "Type" column reads "Worker" and the "Route" column lists "Skilled Worker" and "Health and Care Worker." She confirms the match is current — the file is dated that morning.

The HR manager now knows the licence exists and covers the Health and Care Worker route. She can proceed to the Certificate of Sponsorship stage, which involves a separate allocation request and a per-worker CoS assignment through the SMS. She is also aware that the Immigration Skills Charge will apply — currently up to £1,000 per year per worker for medium or large sponsors, with a reduced rate for small or charitable organisations (verify current rates at GOV.UK before budgeting). For a full explanation of the CoS process, see [Certificate of Sponsorship Explained for Employers 2026](/certificate-of-sponsorship-explained-employers/).

If instead the register had shown no entry even under the registered legal name, the HR manager's next step would be to ask Sunnymead to provide written evidence from their SMS, and if none was forthcoming, to pause the hiring process and advise the candidate accordingly.

## Broader Compliance Context: What Comes After Verification

Confirming a sponsor licence exists is the beginning of due diligence, not the end. HR managers should understand the full shape of their obligations before relying on a licence — their own or that of a company they are working with.

### Right to Work Checks

Separate from the sponsor licence check, UK employers must conduct a statutory right to work check on every employee before employment begins. For sponsored workers, the right to work is established through the visa vignette or the digital status accessible via the Home Office Online Right to Work Checking Service. Conducting a manual document check alone is not sufficient for workers who hold only a digital immigration status. Failure to conduct a compliant check removes the statutory excuse against a civil penalty, which currently runs up to £60,000 per illegal worker (verify current penalty levels at GOV.UK). See [Hiring International Workers: Employer Obligations UK 2026](/hiring-international-workers-employer-obligations/) for a detailed breakdown.

### Salary Thresholds and the Immigration Skills Charge

Sponsoring an overseas worker under the Skilled Worker route requires meeting minimum salary thresholds set by the Home Office, which are route- and occupation-specific. These thresholds are updated periodically. In addition, the Immigration Skills Charge must be paid at the point the CoS is assigned. The charge is not optional and cannot be passed to the worker. Current rates and exemptions are published on the GOV.UK Immigration Skills Charge guidance page.

### Maintaining Your Own Licence's Register Entry

If you are an employer managing your own sponsor licence, remember that your entry on the register is your public-facing proof of status. Any change to your organisation — a change of address, a key contact leaving, a structural acquisition — must be reported to UKVI through the SMS within the specified reporting window. Failure to report changes is one of the most common triggers for compliance visits and can result in downgrading or suspension. This is covered in detail in [Sponsor Licence Compliance Duties After Approval 2026](/sponsor-licence-compliance-duties-after-approval/).

For employers who are in the process of applying, [How to Apply for a UK Skilled Worker Sponsor Licence](/how-to-apply-uk-skilled-worker-sponsor-licence/) sets out the application structure, and the [Sponsor Licence Application Documents Checklist 2026](/sponsor-licence-application-documents-checklist/) covers the supporting evidence UKVI expects to see.

## A Word on Sectors Where Verification Is Especially Critical

The care sector, construction, hospitality, and logistics sectors have all, at various points, seen both legitimate and illegitimate operators claiming sponsorship capability. Workers in these sectors should be especially rigorous about verifying register entries before investing in visa applications.

## When to Involve a Professional

This guide is practical information, not immigration legal advice. Verifying a register entry is something any HR manager or informed candidate can do independently. However, the following situations call for qualified professional input:

- The register check returns an unexpected result and the employer's explanation is unclear.
- You are considering whether a role qualifies for a specific visa route and are uncertain about the occupation code classification.
- A licence has been suspended or revoked and you need to understand the impact on existing employees or pending visa applications.
- You are acquiring a business and need to assess whether the sponsor licence transfers with the corporate structure (it generally does not transfer automatically).

In all these cases, consult a licensed immigration solicitor or an adviser regulated by the Office of the Immigration Services Commissioner (OISC). Do not rely on the employer's own HR team for an independent assessment in a dispute involving that employer.

## Summary Checklist

Before relying on any employer's claim to hold a sponsor licence:

- Download the current Register of Licensed Sponsors: Workers from GOV.UK (check the file date).
- Search by the employer's legal registered name, confirmed via Companies House if needed.
- Confirm the "Type" column shows the correct category (Worker vs. Temporary Worker).
- Confirm the "Route" column includes the specific sub-route relevant to the offered role.
- Note the date you performed the check and retain the file version searched.
- If the result is ambiguous, request written confirmation from the employer's Sponsor Management System.
- Re-check at each significant milestone: before CoS issue, before visa application, before renewal.

The register is a powerful, free, publicly available tool. Using it correctly takes less time than most pre-employment checks. The cost of not using it, for both employers and workers, can be considerably higher.
