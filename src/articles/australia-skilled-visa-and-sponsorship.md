*Figures checked 11 November 2026. Income thresholds are indexed every 1 July.*

Australia runs two parallel skilled migration systems that demand entirely different work from you. Knowing which one you are in — and ideally running both — matters more than any single piece of advice about either.

## Two systems

**Points-tested.** You arrange a skills assessment, sit an English test, submit an Expression of Interest through SkillSelect, and wait to be invited. No employer needed. Your occupation must be on the relevant skilled occupation list. If invited, the visa is permanent from grant, or in regional categories provisional with a defined path to permanence.

**Employer-sponsored.** An Australian business nominates you for a specific position. The business must be an approved sponsor, the role genuine, and the salary must clear a published threshold. The main temporary route is the Skills in Demand visa, subclass 482, leading to permanent residence through subclass 186.

They fail for different reasons, which is why you run both. A points application fails because your score is not competitive in your occupation. A sponsored application fails because no employer will absorb the cost. Neither predicts the other.

## The Core Skills Income Threshold

From **1 July 2026** the Core Skills Income Threshold is **AUD 79,423**, after an indexation increase of **3.8 per cent**. It applies to subclasses **482, 494 and 186**.

**The salary must clear both tests** — at least the threshold, *and* at least the annual market salary rate, meaning what an Australian doing the same job in the same place would be paid.

**It is indexed every 1 July.** A nomination lodged on 28 June and one lodged on 2 July are tested against different numbers. Where a salary sits near the line, lodgement timing is a financial decision.

A higher **Specialist Skills Income Threshold** applies to a faster processing stream for high-earning roles. Figures around AUD 146,576 have been widely reported; treat that as indicative and confirm with the department, because its salary page does not render its figures reliably.

## The points test

**Age** is the largest factor you cannot influence. The top award sits in the 25 to 32 band, declines from 33, and falls sharply after 40 — and it gets worse while you wait.

**English.** The gap between competent, proficient and superior is substantial, and moving from proficient to superior is usually the cheapest points available. Native speakers still sit an approved test, and the writing component catches people out.

**Skilled employment.** Years of relevant post-qualification experience, counted separately for work inside and outside Australia.

**Qualifications.** Doctorate, bachelor's degree, or diploma or trade qualification.

**Partner.** Points for a partner with competent English and a positive skills assessment, and a separate award if you have no partner or a partner who is already Australian. A partner sitting an English test can be worth more than another year of your own experience.

**Smaller awards** for community language credentials, regional study, and a professional year.

The score you need is not fixed. There is a floor to submit an Expression of Interest, but the score that actually receives an invitation depends on who else is waiting in your occupation. Occupations in genuine shortage are invited near the floor. Crowded ones — accounting, several IT categories — have required far more.

## Start with the skills assessment

Your occupation must be assessed by the designated assessing authority for that occupation. Engineers, accountants, nurses and trades go to different bodies, each with its own evidence requirements, fee and timeline.

This is the step people leave last and should start first. It routinely takes months, frequently comes back requesting more documents, and blocks everything downstream.

Assessments have validity periods, commonly two to three years, so one that expires mid-process has to be redone.

## State and territory nomination

Every state and territory runs its own programme, and most applicants underuse them.

A nomination adds points and, in several categories, is the difference between an invitation and an indefinite wait. Each state publishes its own occupation list — **not** the national list — with its own criteria, often a commitment to live and work in the state.

The states are not competing for the same people. An occupation hopelessly oversubscribed nationally may be in real shortage in South Australia or Tasmania.

Programmes open and close through the year with finite allocations and little notice. Set up alerts.

## The regional routes

**Subclass 491**, Skilled Work Regional, is points-tested and requires state nomination or sponsorship by an eligible relative in a designated regional area. Five years, with a residence condition, leading to permanence through subclass 191.

**Subclass 494**, Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional, is employer-sponsored, five years, also leading to 191, and subject to the Core Skills Income Threshold.

"Regional" is broader than it sounds and includes substantial cities. The conditions are real and monitored — people have had visas cancelled for moving to a capital city. The upside is extra points, more eligible occupations, far less competition, and a cost of living that makes AUD 80,000 go much further than it would in Sydney.

## How the Expression of Interest works

Submission is free and is not an application. Invitation rounds go to the highest scorers in each occupation, and where two tie the earlier submission wins — which is why submitting as soon as you are eligible matters even if you expect to improve later.

An Expression of Interest is generally valid for two years and can be updated.

**Two mistakes.** Claiming points you cannot evidence: claims are verified at application stage, and an unsupported claim can lead to refusal and an adverse finding about your conduct. And treating the invitation as the finish line: you usually have sixty days to lodge a complete application with all evidence, and starting police certificates after the invitation is how invitations expire.

## Why applications fail

**Points claimed without evidence.** The most serious category, because the finding follows you.

**Employment references in the wrong format.** Both assessing authorities and the department specify what a reference must contain: job title, exact dates, hours per week, and duties described in enough detail to match the occupation.

**Skilled employment counted from the wrong date.** Experience usually counts only from the point you met the entry requirements for the occupation.

**Health requirement surprises.** The requirement applies to every member of the family unit, including those not migrating with you.

**Occupation drift.** Your title, your duties and the occupation you nominate must align.

## What the sponsored route asks of the employer

Three parts have to line up. The business must be or become an **approved sponsor**. It must **nominate** the position, showing the role is genuine, in an eligible occupation, and paid at both the threshold and the market rate. You then lodge the **visa application**.

Employers also contribute to the Skilling Australians Fund as part of nomination. That levy is an employer cost and cannot lawfully be passed to you. An employer suggesting you cover it is telling you something about how they handle obligations generally.

Ask early whether they already hold sponsor approval — an employer starting from zero adds months.

## After you arrive

**Apply for a tax file number immediately**, or your employer must withhold at the top marginal rate.

**Check your Medicare position.** Permanent residents and certain temporary holders from countries with a reciprocal agreement are eligible. Most temporary holders are not and are generally required to hold private health insurance as a visa condition — a gap can breach it.

**Understand superannuation.** Your employer pays a percentage of salary into a fund, claimable on permanent departure from a temporary visa at a high tax rate.

**Expect no credit history.** Budget for larger rental deposits.

## The order to do things in

1. Confirm your occupation on a current list — national **and** state — and identify the assessing authority.
2. Sit your English test; if near a band boundary, sit it again.
3. Start the skills assessment. Expect months.
4. Calculate your points honestly, including partner points.
5. Submit the Expression of Interest; approach states in parallel.
6. Run the sponsored job search simultaneously.
7. Order police certificates early.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a job offer?**
Not for points-tested routes. Yes for employer-sponsored routes.

**How long does it take?**
Long. Skills assessment alone is often several months, and the invitation wait is unpredictable.

**Does age really matter that much?**
Yes. It is the largest factor you cannot change.

**Can my partner help my application?**
Yes. Competent English plus a positive skills assessment adds points.

**Is subclass 482 permanent?**
No, temporary, with pathways to permanence most commonly through subclass 186.

**Can my employer ask me to pay the nomination levy?**
No. It is a sponsor cost.

**What if my occupation is not listed?**
Check state lists as well as the national one, and consider adjacent occupations your experience supports.

**Do I need private health insurance?**
Usually yes on a temporary visa, and frequently as a visa condition.

## Health and character

Every applicant undergoes a health examination with a panel physician, and the health requirement applies to the **whole family unit**, including members who are not migrating with you. The test is concerned with cost to the public health system and public health risk, not with whether you are well enough to work.

Character requires police certificates from every country where you have lived for a qualifying period, plus a declaration. Old convictions do not automatically disqualify, but non-disclosure is treated far more seriously than most underlying matters. Declare everything, including matters that did not result in a conviction where the question asks about them.

Both requirements tend to surface late, because they are usually addressed after an invitation when the sixty-day clock is already running. Order police certificates before you are invited, not after.

## Points and sponsored routes compared

| | Points-tested | Employer-sponsored |
|---|---|---|
| Employer needed | No | Yes |
| Status on grant | Permanent (or provisional, regional) | Temporary, with a path to permanence |
| Main bottleneck | Assessment, then invitation competition | A sponsor at or above the threshold |
| Age | Scored heavily | Not scored the same way |
| Timeline control | Mostly yours | Mostly the employer's |
| Changing jobs | Freely | Not without a new nomination |

## Comparing Australia with the alternatives

For someone weighing destinations, the honest position is this.

**Against the UK**, Australia's advantage is that you can start without an employer and the status granted on the points routes is permanent from day one. Its disadvantage is the time — a skills assessment plus an invitation wait makes it slower to begin, where a UK offer can move from Certificate of Sponsorship to arrival in weeks.

**Against Canada**, the two systems are structurally similar, both points-based with provincial or state nomination layered on. Canada's draws have at times targeted specific occupation groups, which can let a mid-scoring candidate through. Australia weights age more heavily.

**Against the US**, Australia's whole process is predictable where the H-1B lottery is not. You can calculate your points. You cannot calculate your odds in a lottery.

Nobody has to choose one. An Expression of Interest costs nothing to submit and sits for two years while you pursue other routes.
