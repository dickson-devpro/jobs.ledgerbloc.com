*Figures checked 11 November 2026 against GOV.UK.*

There is no general UK work visa you can apply for on your own merits. No points test you can sit, no expression of interest to lodge, no pool to wait in while you improve your score.

With narrow exceptions, entry to the UK labour market runs through one door: a British employer, holding a Home Office licence, offering you a named job at or above a published salary. Everything in this guide follows from that.

## The structure

An employer applies to the Home Office for a sponsor licence, proving the business is genuine and operating, has HR systems capable of meeting sponsor duties, and has suitable people in the key roles.

Once licensed, the employer assigns you a **Certificate of Sponsorship** — not a document despite the name, but a record on a government database with a reference number carrying the job title, occupation code, salary and dates.

You then apply using that number, and separately satisfy the salary requirement, the English requirement, the maintenance requirement and the general suitability requirements.

If granted, your permission is tied to that employer and that job. Change either and you need a new certificate and a new application.

## The salary requirement

The general threshold is **£41,700 per year**.

But the test is not "above £41,700". You must be paid the **higher** of:

- £41,700, or
- the published going rate for your specific occupation code.

If the going rate for your occupation is £46,000 and the offer is £43,000, you fail — even though £43,000 clears the general figure. Going rates are published per occupation code and there are hundreds of them. The employer's HR team knows which code they intend to use; ask in the first conversation, because the code sets the number you actually have to beat.

A lower threshold of **£33,400** applies in certain cases outside healthcare and education.

Prison officers switching or extending have a separate figure of £31,300, with certificate deadlines of **31 December 2026** for switching and **31 December 2027** for extending.

Extensions in certain medium-skilled occupations are available only where the first Certificate of Sponsorship was issued before **22 July 2025**. Older guides listing a wider range of eligible jobs predate that cut-off.

### The £44,000 figure is not the law

A large amount of published material quotes £44,000 as the threshold. That number came from a salary requirements review published in 2026 — a **recommendation**, not a rule, and it is not in the Immigration Rules.

If a recruiter quotes you £44,000, they are working from secondary sources. Plan against £41,700 and the going rate for your code.

| Immigration Health Surcharge | £1,035 per person per year |
| Maintenance funds | £1,270, held 28 consecutive days |

### The health surcharge is the number people miss

**£1,035 per person per year**, charged for every applicant, for every year of the visa, payable in full at the point of application.

A five-year visa for one adult is £5,175 in surcharge alone. A family of four on a five-year visa is £20,700 before the visa fees.

What it buys is NHS access on the same basis as a resident — no insurance, no claims, no per-visit charges. It is still a very large payment before you have earned anything.

**A note on sources.** GOV.UK's own migrant health guide has at times continued to display older surcharge rates of £624 and £470. Those are out of date. Take the figure from the Skilled Worker cost page, not the health guide.

### The maintenance clock

You must have held at least **£1,270** for **28 consecutive days**, with day 28 falling within 31 days of your application.

This is the single most common technical refusal on this route. Money transferred in last week does not count, however large the balance. A dip below £1,270 on day 19 restarts the 28 days.

Your employer can instead certify on the Certificate of Sponsorship that it will cover your costs for the first month up to £1,270, which removes the requirement entirely. Most sponsors will do it if asked, and most candidates never ask.

### Worked example

Single applicant, three-year visa, from outside the UK:

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Application fee | £819 |
| Health surcharge, 3 years | £3,105 |
| **Subtotal** | **£3,924** |

Add a spouse and it roughly doubles. Add two children and it roughly doubles again.

Nothing obliges the employer to pay any of it. Raise it at offer stage — before accepting, not after.

## English language

You must demonstrate English at level B1, through an approved Secure English Language Test, a degree taught in English, or exemption by nationality.

Nationals of a defined list of majority English-speaking countries are exempt. **Nationality** exempts you, not residence or where you studied. If your nationality is not on the list but your degree was taught in English, you will generally rely on the degree route, which may require confirmation from UK ENIC that the qualification is equivalent to a UK degree.

## Finding a licensed sponsor

**Check the register first.** The Home Office publishes a register of licensed sponsors. Search the exact legal name — businesses trade under names differing from their registered entity. Check the routes they hold, because a licence for one route does not cover another. Check the rating, because a sponsor on an action plan may be unable to issue new certificates.

If the employer is not on the register, the job is not available to you unless they apply for a licence — months and money, and most will not do it for one hire.

**Target sectors that sponsor.** Healthcare and social care, higher education and research, specialist engineering, technology at scale, finance, professional services with international clients. Retail, hospitality, small practices and most businesses under fifty people rarely do.

**Work out your floor first.** Find your occupation code, look up the going rate, and target roles at a seniority that can pay it. Junior applications are usually futile.

**Say it in the first line.** "I would need sponsorship under the Skilled Worker route." Employers who cannot sponsor will tell you immediately.

## Dependants

Partner and children under 18 can apply as dependants. Each pays the full fee and the full health surcharge.

A dependent partner generally has the right to work, with some occupational restrictions, which changes household finances substantially.

Children born in the UK to parents who both hold temporary permission do **not** automatically acquire British citizenship. The UK has not had unconditional birthright citizenship since 1983.

## Settlement is changing

The route has historically led to settlement after five years of continuous lawful residence, subject to absence limits and a knowledge-of-life test.

A government response published in late 2026 set out an intention to move the standard qualifying period from five to ten years. At the time of writing that is **not yet in force**, and the position for people already here is among the open questions.

If settlement is why you are moving, follow this closely and take advice on your own circumstances. Watch the absence limits either way — people lose eligibility by accepting an overseas assignment without checking.

## Routes without a sponsor

**Global Talent.** Endorsement by a recognised body in academia, research, arts and culture, or digital technology instead of a job offer. Not tied to an employer.

**High Potential Individual.** Two years unsponsored for recent graduates of a defined list of overseas universities.

**Innovator Founder.** For people establishing an innovative business, with endorsement from an approved body.

**Health and Care Worker visa.** A variant for eligible health and care roles with reduced fees and **exemption from the Immigration Health Surcharge**. If your occupation qualifies, check this before anything else — it is dramatically cheaper.

**Family routes.** Separate entirely. The minimum income requirement for a partner remains **£29,000** — the announced increases to £34,500 and £38,700 were never brought into force, and a great deal of published material is wrong about this.

## Three questions before accepting

**What occupation code will the certificate use, and what is its going rate?**

**Will you cover the visa fee and health surcharge, including dependants?**

**What happens to my status if the role ends in the first year?** Sponsored permission does not survive the end of sponsorship indefinitely, and the window is short.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I move to the UK without a job offer?**
Only via Global Talent, High Potential Individual, Innovator Founder, or a family route.

**How long does the visa take?**
The Home Office publishes a service standard, which is indicative and changes through the year.

**Can I switch employers?**
Yes, but the new employer must be a licensed sponsor and must assign a new certificate, and you must apply to update your visa before starting.

**Is the health surcharge refundable?**
Partial refunds are available in defined circumstances. You must claim it.

**Do I need my qualifications assessed?**
Not generally for the visa. Your profession may require registration with a UK regulator before you can practise.

**Can I bring my parents?**
Not as dependants. The adult dependent relative rules are extremely restrictive.

**What is a share code?**
A code from the government's view-and-prove service letting a landlord, employer or bank check your status online.

**Is there an upper age limit?**
No, unlike points-based systems elsewhere.

## Your first month in the UK

The admin looks familiar and almost none of it works the way you expect.

**Your status is digital.** The UK has moved from physical biometric residence permits to an online record. You prove your status with a share code generated through the government's view-and-prove service. Set up your UKVI account before a letting agent asks for it.

**National Insurance number.** The UK equivalent of a social security number. You can usually start work before it arrives, but apply immediately because it affects your tax code — an emergency code costs you money every month until corrected.

**Register with a GP.** You have paid the health surcharge, but access is mediated through a general practice. Registering takes a short form and proof of address. Do it before you are ill.

**Banking.** A UK account generally requires proof of address, and proof of address generally requires an account or a tenancy. The ways through are an employer letter confirming your address and start date, a bank with a newcomer process, or an app-based bank with lighter onboarding.

**Credit history.** Yours does not transfer. Register on the electoral roll if eligible and expect larger deposits in the first year.

**Driving.** Licences from some countries can be exchanged; others cannot, and you take the theory and practical tests. Waiting lists have been long.

**Council tax, TV licence and utilities** start the moment you move in, and none of them finds you automatically.

## What to check on the Certificate of Sponsorship

When the reference number arrives, check every field before you use it. Errors here propagate into the visa application and are tedious to correct afterwards.

- Your name exactly as it appears in your passport, including middle names
- Passport number and nationality
- Job title and the **occupation code**
- Gross annual salary, and whether it matches the offer
- Start and end dates
- Work address, which must be where you will actually work
- Whether the employer has certified maintenance

A mismatch between the certificate and your passport is one of the simplest ways to have an application delayed, and it is entirely preventable with five minutes of checking.

## Switching inside the UK

If you are already in the UK on another route — a student visa, a graduate visa, a dependant visa — switching to Skilled Worker is usually possible without leaving.

Two practical advantages. The employer is exempt from the Immigration Skills Charge where you switch from a study visa and then extend, which makes you materially cheaper to hire and is worth saying explicitly in an application. And you avoid entry clearance, so you can start sooner.

Two cautions. The inside-the-UK fee is higher — £943 for up to three years against £819 from outside. And some routes carry conditions preventing switching, so check your current permission before planning around it.

## Tax, if you are moving from the United States

The US taxes its citizens on worldwide income regardless of residence. Moving to the UK does not end your US filing obligation; it adds a second one.

Relief from double taxation comes through the US-UK treaty and through mechanisms such as the foreign earned income exclusion and foreign tax credits — but these require filing to claim, and the choice between them has long-term consequences that are awkward to reverse.

Complications that catch people: foreign account reporting obligations that apply to ordinary current accounts, not just investments; the technical treatment of UK workplace pensions under US rules; and the fact that ISAs and several common UK investment products are not recognised as tax-free by the IRS and can create punitive reporting.

Engage a cross-border accountant before your first filing season rather than after.
