*Figures checked 11 November 2026 against GOV.UK.*

Hiring from overseas is not a more complicated version of hiring locally. It is a different process with a licensing step at the front, a different cost base, and a compliance regime that outlasts the hire by years.

## The licence comes first, and it changes your timeline

A UK employer cannot sponsor a skilled worker without first holding a sponsor licence. The licence belongs to the organisation, not to a role, and obtaining one means demonstrating that you are a genuine operating business with HR systems capable of meeting sponsor duties and suitable people in the key roles.

The budget implication is modest. The **timeline** implication is not. If you have found an outstanding candidate and do not hold a licence, the candidate will usually be gone before you are ready. Employers who hire internationally with any regularity get the licence first and recruit second.

Licence fees vary by organisation size and route and are revised periodically. Take the current figure from the government's fees table.

## The Immigration Skills Charge

| Sponsor type | First 12 months | Each additional 6 months |
|---|---|---|
| Small or charitable sponsor | £480 | £240 |
| Medium or large sponsor | £1,320 | £660 |

Over five years: **£2,400** small or charitable, **£6,600** medium or large.

**It is paid in full, up front.** When you assign the Certificate of Sponsorship you pay the entire charge for the entire period in one payment.

**There is no six-month rate.** More than six months but under a year means you pay the full twelve.

**Dependants are free.** The charge applies to the worker only — a sponsored worker bringing a partner and three children generates no extra charge.

**A job change costs only the additional time.** A new certificate for an existing worker attracts the charge only on any period beyond what you already paid for.

## The exemptions most employers never check

**Exempt occupation codes: 2111, 2112, 2113, 2114, 2115, 2119, 2161, 2162 and 2311.** These cover scientists, research and development managers, researchers and higher education teaching professionals.

If you are a university, a research institute or an R&D-intensive business, check your codes before budgeting anything. The difference between £6,600 and nothing is not a rounding error.

**Certificates assigned before 6 April 2017.** The charge may not apply.

**Study-to-work switchers.** No charge where the worker switches from a study visa to a Skilled Worker or Senior or Specialist Worker visa and then extends.

This is the most commercially useful exemption in the system. Recruiting from the pool of international graduates already in the country removes the largest fixed cost of sponsorship, and those candidates can start immediately with no entry clearance wait.

**Certain Global Business Mobility transfers**, where the certificate was assigned on or after 1 January 2023, the worker is an EU national or holds a Latvian non-citizen's passport, they normally work in the EU for your EU business, and the transfer is no longer than 36 months.

## Salary is the real cost driver

A sponsored skilled worker must be paid the higher of **£41,700 per year** and the published going rate for the occupation code. A lower threshold of **£33,400** applies in certain cases outside healthcare and education.

**You cannot sponsor below the threshold**, even where that is the genuine market rate in your region. The threshold is national.

**The occupation code is a budget decision.** If the going rate exceeds the general threshold, the going rate becomes your floor. Choose honestly — classifying a role to reach a lower going rate is a compliance failure, and compliance failures cost licences.

## Worker costs you may absorb

- Application fee, **£819** up to three years from outside the UK, **£1,618** for more
- Immigration Health Surcharge, **£1,035 per person per year**, up front for the full length
- Maintenance of **£1,270** held 28 consecutive days, unless you certify their first month

Nothing requires you to pay these. But a candidate with a family faces a five-figure bill before earning anything, and for many roles that decides whether they accept.

The distinction matters: the Immigration Skills Charge **cannot** be passed to the worker. The visa fee and health surcharge **may** be absorbed by you as a recruitment decision.

## Right to work checks are separate

Every employer must check that every employee has the right to work, regardless of nationality, **before** employment begins. Doing it correctly gives a statutory excuse against a civil penalty if permission later proves invalid.

Most people with time-limited permission now evidence it through an online share code checked via the government service. Manual checks remain for some categories.

Two things catch employers out: the check must happen **before** the first day, not during onboarding week; and for time-limited permission there is a follow-up obligation when it expires. A calendar reminder is the whole system.

## The duties that outlast the hire

**Record keeping.** Specified documents for each sponsored worker, including recruitment evidence where required, contact details, absence records and a copy of their permission.

**Reporting.** Certain events reported through the sponsor management system within set deadlines, typically ten or twenty working days: a worker failing to start, leaving early, a significant change in duties, a change of work location, and changes to the organisation itself.

**Monitoring.** You must be able to show you know where your sponsored workers are and that they are doing the job on the certificate.

**Cooperation.** Access for compliance purposes, including unannounced visits.

## What a compliance visit involves

An officer typically wants your HR systems, files for sampled sponsored workers, evidence of right-to-work checks, and proof that you monitor attendance and report changes. They may interview sponsored workers and their managers to test whether the role performed matches the role described.

The common findings are mundane: records in several places and never reconciled; a change noticed internally but never reported; checks completed after the start date; a worker doing a materially different job from the one on the certificate.

Consequences run from a warning, through downgrading to a B-rating with a chargeable action plan, to suspension and revocation. **Revocation curtails the permission of every sponsored worker you employ.** That belongs on an operational risk register, not in an HR folder.

## Check whether you need to sponsor at all

**People who already hold unrestricted permission.** Settled status, indefinite leave, dependants of other visa holders, graduates on post-study routes, people on family routes. An ordinary right-to-work check and you hire them. This pool is larger than most employers assume and is routinely screened out by an application form asking whether the candidate needs sponsorship — which many do not.

**International graduates already here.** Cheaper, because of the study-to-work exemption, and available immediately.

**An employer of record in the worker's own country**, where the work genuinely can be done remotely. Not a workaround for a role requiring presence, and it carries tax and permanent establishment questions.

**Restructuring the role.** A role that cannot clear the threshold as advertised can sometimes legitimately be scoped at a higher level with a corresponding salary — where the business need supports it, and never as a paper exercise.

## Building the internal business case

**Present the five-year figure.** The skills charge is paid up front for the whole period, so a first-year number is wrong and will be found to be wrong.

**Separate mandatory from discretionary.** Licence, certificate fee and skills charge are costs of doing this at all. Visa fee and health surcharge are a recruitment decision. Presenting them as one number invites refusal on the total.

**Quantify the alternative.** The comparison is not sponsorship versus a free local hire; it is sponsorship versus the vacancy staying open. A role open eight months has already cost more than the five-year sponsorship bill.

**Name the compliance owner and the training budget.**

**Flag the renewal date.** Licences are granted for a defined period and must be renewed.

## Refunds nobody claims

The Immigration Skills Charge is partially refundable where the visa application is refused or withdrawn, the worker never takes up the role, or the worker leaves before the end of the period paid for — in which case a refund may be due for unused full six-month blocks.

Refunds are not automatic and have time limits. If you sponsor at any volume, put a refund check into the leavers process.

## A five-year budget

Medium or large sponsor, excluding salary:

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Immigration Skills Charge, 5 years | £6,600 |
| Certificate of Sponsorship fee | per current fees table |
| Sponsor licence, apportioned | per current fees table |
| Compliance administration | internal staff time |
| Optional: worker's visa fee absorbed | £1,618 |
| Optional: health surcharge absorbed, 5 years | £5,175 |

An employer covering the worker's costs is looking at well over £13,000 across five years for one hire, before dependants and before salary.

## Outside the UK

**Australia.** Approved sponsor status, nomination of the position, and the **Core Skills Income Threshold of AUD 79,423** from 1 July 2026 for subclasses 482, 494 and 186, alongside the market salary rate. Sponsors contribute to the Skilling Australians Fund, and that levy cannot be recovered from the worker.

**United States.** Filing fees, often a labour condition or certification process, and attorney costs exceeding the filing fees. Certain costs are legally the employer's. The H-1B runs on an annual cap with a registration lottery, so your ability to hire is constrained by chance rather than budget.

The constants: a licensing step before you can hire at all, a wage floor you cannot negotiate below, some costs that are legally yours, and duties continuing for the life of the employment.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I pass the Immigration Skills Charge to the worker?**
No. It is an employer cost.

**Do I pay it for dependants?**
No, the worker only.

**Is it refundable if the worker leaves early?**
Partially, in defined circumstances. You have to claim it.

**Do I need a licence to hire someone who already has the right to work?**
No. An ordinary right-to-work check is sufficient.

**Do I have to advertise locally first?**
The resident labour market test was abolished for the Skilled Worker route. Genuineness of the vacancy is still assessed.

**Can a small business get a licence?**
Yes, and small and charitable sponsors pay the lower charge. The obstacle is the compliance burden.

**What counts as a small sponsor?**
Company-size tests based on turnover, balance sheet total and employee numbers.

**Do I pay again on an extension?**
Yes, for the additional period.

## What this guide will not give you

A current sponsor licence fee, because it varies by organisation size and route and is revised periodically — take it from the government's fees table on the day you apply.

Processing times, because published service standards move through the year.

Any figure for how likely a licence application is to succeed, because no meaningful statistic is published.

And no "average" cost of sponsoring a worker, because the range between an exempt-code university hire and a medium-sponsor five-year hire with absorbed worker costs runs from roughly £0 to £13,000 for the same length of employment. An average would be true of nobody.

## Where the licence application itself goes wrong

The common refusal grounds are procedural rather than substantive, which means they are avoidable.

**No genuine trading presence evidenced.** The business must demonstrate it is lawfully operating in the UK, with the documents specified for its type.

**Key personnel who do not meet the suitability requirements.** The authorising officer and level one user have to satisfy specific criteria, and an unsuitable appointment can sink an otherwise solid application.

**HR systems that cannot meet the duties.** A pre-licence compliance visit will test whether you can actually monitor attendance, record absences and report changes. "We will build that once we are licensed" is not an answer.

**Inconsistency between the application and public records.** Registered address, directors, company status. Reconcile these before applying.

**A vacancy that does not look genuine.** A role created for a specific person, or described in terms lifted from that person's CV, invites scrutiny.

## Practical first steps for an employer new to this

1. Decide whether you genuinely need to sponsor, or whether the candidate already has permission to work.
2. Identify the occupation code and check whether it is on the exempt list for the skills charge.
3. Look up the going rate for that code and confirm your salary clears both it and the general threshold.
4. Name an internal owner for sponsor compliance and budget for their training.
5. Apply for the licence, before you recruit.
6. Build your record-keeping and reporting process before the first hire, not after.
7. Diarise the licence renewal date and every sponsored worker's permission expiry.

## The cheapest hire available

Worth stating plainly, because it is the single most actionable point for an employer new to this.

An international graduate already in the UK on a study visa, switching to Skilled Worker, costs you **no Immigration Skills Charge at all** — and can usually start within weeks rather than months, because there is no entry clearance wait.

Compare that with sponsoring the same person from overseas: £1,320 for the first year and £660 per additional six months for a medium or large sponsor, plus the entry clearance timeline.

If your recruitment process screens out candidates at the application form on the question "do you require sponsorship?", you are discarding the cheapest sponsored hires in the market alongside the most expensive ones, without distinguishing between them.
