*The H-1B fee litigation position was checked on 11 November 2026. This is live litigation with appeals pending in two circuits — verify before relying on it.*

## The United States: a blocked fee and a live lottery

The main route is the H-1B specialty occupation visa — employer-sponsored, running on an annual cap, with entry by registration and random selection. The cap is the constraint. Registrations have substantially exceeded available places in recent years, so a strong candidate with a willing employer can simply not be selected.

### Where the $100,000 fee stands

A presidential proclamation in September 2025 imposed a $100,000 payment requirement in connection with certain H-1B petitions. A proclamation of **18 September 2026** extended it through **21 September 2027**.

The courts have not gone along with it:

- **State of California v. Mullin** (D. Mass.) — on **8 June 2026** the court held the fee an unlawful tax and vacated the policy. On **24 July 2026** the First Circuit declined to stay that ruling pending appeal.
- **Global Nurse Force v. Trump** (N.D. Cal.) — on **30 September 2026** the court blocked the agency policies implementing both the original proclamation and the extension, pending proper rulemaking. The government appealed to the Ninth Circuit on **9 October 2026**.
- **Chamber of Commerce v. DHS** (D.D.C.) — upheld in December 2025; on appeal, argued in the D.C. Circuit in March 2026.

As at the date of this check, employers are **not being required to pay** the fee, and USCIS has indicated it will comply with the court orders.

Separately, a DHS proposed rule of **25 August 2026** would create a fee of **$103,265** on cap-subject H-1B petitions. That is a proposal, not in force.

**How to treat it.** Appeals are pending in two circuits and a rulemaking is underway. The position can change. Do not plan on the assumption the fee will never apply, and do not accept an employer's assurance either way without a current check.

### Routes that avoid the lottery

**O-1A**, extraordinary ability, no cap, used by senior engineers more than people realise.

**L-1 intracompany transfer**, if your employer has a US entity and you have the qualifying service.

**TN**, for Canadian and Mexican nationals in certain occupations.

**Cap-exempt employers** — universities and affiliated non-profits — can file outside the lottery at any time of year.

## The United Kingdom: predictable, with a hard floor

No cap, no lottery, which makes the UK more predictable than the US. The constraint is a licensed sponsor and a salary threshold.

The general threshold is **£41,700 per year**, or the going rate for your occupation code, whichever is higher. A lower threshold of **£33,400** applies in certain cases outside healthcare and education.

This works in London and the larger tech centres and is marginal elsewhere. A mid-level role in a smaller UK city may genuinely pay below the threshold, making it unsponsorable regardless of enthusiasm on either side.

**Costs.** Visa fee from outside the UK is **£819** for up to three years, **£1,618** for more. Immigration Health Surcharge **£1,035 per person per year**, up front. Maintenance **£1,270** held 28 consecutive days unless the employer certifies your first month. A single developer on a three-year visa faces roughly £3,900; with a partner and child, well over £11,000.

**Unsponsored alternatives.** Global Talent, via endorsement by a recognised body in digital technology, is not tied to an employer. High Potential Individual gives two years unsponsored to recent graduates of a defined list of overseas universities.

## Australia: start without an employer

**The points route.** Expression of Interest, invitation, application. No employer needed. IT occupations have historically been crowded, so the invited score can sit well above the eligibility floor. You need a skills assessment from the designated assessing authority, and that is the slow step.

**The sponsored route.** Skills in Demand, subclass 482, requires an approved sponsor and a salary meeting the **Core Skills Income Threshold of AUD 79,423** from **1 July 2026**, plus the market salary rate. Indexed every 1 July.

**Age is the first thing to check.** Points fall sharply after 32 and again after 40.

**State nomination is underused.** State lists differ from the national list, and an IT occupation hopeless nationally may be in shortage in a particular state.

## Canada: the one most developers skip

**Express Entry** is a points-based route to **permanent residence**. Candidates receive a Comprehensive Ranking System score based on age, education, language and work experience, and the highest scorers in each draw are invited. No job offer required, and the status granted is permanent — not a temporary visa needing conversion.

**Category-based draws** target specific occupation groups and have at times included STEM occupations. A developer whose general score would not clear a standard draw may be invited in a category draw at a materially lower score.

**The Global Skills Strategy** offers expedited work permit processing for certain high-skilled occupations where an employer is hiring.

The trade-off is compensation, generally lower than equivalent US roles. But for permanent status without a lottery and without being tied to an employer, it is the most direct route in North America.

## The transfer nobody tries first

If you work for a company with an office in your target country, stop reading job boards.

Transfer routes exist everywhere — the L-1 in the US, the Senior or Specialist Worker route in the UK, equivalents in Australia and Canada. They typically require a qualifying period with the group, often twelve months, and a move into a comparable-level role.

The employer already has the entity, usually already has the licence, already knows you, and runs no recruitment process. The decision is internal and can be made by one or two people. Many transfers happen within weeks.

Trigger it by telling your manager you would be interested in a role in that office if one came up — not by applying externally to your own company.

## The salary reality by market

**United Kingdom.** £41,700, or the going rate if higher, sits above what many non-London mid-level developer roles pay. London, or a senior role elsewhere, is the realistic target. A developer with three years' experience applying to a regional UK company will frequently find the salary cannot clear the floor.

**Australia.** AUD 79,423 is more forgiving against local salaries, so the binding constraint is the points competition. That flips the strategy — your effort goes into the skills assessment and the English band, not the job search.

**United States.** The prevailing wage requirement operates by occupation and geography, but the lottery dominates. A candidate who would clear the wage test comfortably can still be excluded by chance.

**Canada.** Express Entry has no salary floor, because it is not employer-dependent. The constraint is your score, which is why Canada suits people strong on age, education and language even with a modest current salary.

## Which specialisms travel

Those with the thinnest global supply:

- distributed systems and large-scale infrastructure
- security engineering, particularly with a compliance dimension
- machine learning infrastructure, as distinct from model work
- embedded and real-time systems
- anything with a regulatory overlay — payments, health data, aviation, automotive safety

Generalist web development is sponsorable everywhere and faces the deepest local competition everywhere, which means the salary threshold does more of the filtering.

This is about supply, not fashion. A COBOL developer with genuine mainframe experience is often easier to sponsor than a generalist with a modern stack, because the pool of local alternatives is close to empty.

## Building your own evidence

**For an O-1A or Global Talent endorsement**, the evidence *is* the application: original contributions of major significance, published material about your work, a record of judging others' work, membership of associations requiring outstanding achievement, high remuneration relative to the field, authorship of technical articles.

Most developers assume none applies and are wrong about at least two. Maintaining a widely used open source project is an original contribution. Reviewing conference submissions is judging. Writing cited by others is published material. The gap is usually not the achievement but the evidence — nobody keeps the download statistics, the citations, the invitations.

Start a folder now. An application built from three years of saved evidence is a different document from one assembled in six weeks from memory.

**For an employer-sponsored route**, what reduces an employer's uncertainty is public work they can inspect: a repository with real commit history, a product with users, a talk recording, writing that shows how you reason.

**For a points-based route**, the evidence is procedural: employment references in the specified format with exact dates and hours, qualification documents, and an English test at the highest band you can reach.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the $100,000 H-1B fee being charged?**
Not as at 11 November 2026. Vacated in one case, implementing policies blocked in another, appeals pending.

**Can I get to the US without the lottery?**
Yes — O-1A, L-1, TN for Canadians and Mexicans, or a cap-exempt employer.

**Do I need a computer science degree?**
For the H-1B, the specialty occupation test generally expects a relevant degree or an equivalent combination of education and experience.

**Can I go to Australia without a job offer?**
Yes, through the points-tested routes.

**Which route is fastest?**
Usually an internal transfer at a company you already work for.

**Does remote work for a foreign company give me a visa?**
No, and doing it from a country on a tourist visa generally breaches that visa's conditions.

**Should I accept a lower salary to get sponsored?**
Salary floors exist precisely to prevent that.

**Is an O-1A realistic for an ordinary engineer?**
More often than people assume, but it rewards years of saved evidence rather than a sprint.

## Questions worth asking an employer

**Have you sponsored before, and who handles it internally?** A company with a named person who has done this is a very different proposition from one working it out between other tasks.

**What occupation code will you use, and what is its going rate?** In the UK this determines whether the offer is sponsorable at all.

**Will you cover the visa fee and health charge, including dependants?** Frequently negotiable, almost never volunteered. On a five-year UK visa for a family of four the health surcharge alone is £20,700.

**What is the realistic start date?** Employers new to this routinely underestimate by months.

**What happens if the role is made redundant in the first year?** Sponsored permission does not survive the end of sponsorship indefinitely, and the window to find another sponsor is short.

## What this guide will not claim

There is no reliable published figure for how long any of these routes takes in practice, because published service standards move and exclude cases sent for further checks.

There are no trustworthy demand figures for developers by country — the numbers in circulation come from recruitment firms with a commercial interest in the answer.

And nobody can estimate your odds in the H-1B lottery, because they depend entirely on how many other people register.

What is solid: the salary thresholds, the structure of each route, and the current status of the fee litigation. Check everything else on the day.

## If you are early in your career

All four systems filter by seniority through salary floors, which means the honest advice for a developer with one or two years' experience is usually that none of them is available yet.

The realistic paths are:

**Study abroad, then convert.** A master's degree in the destination country, followed by a post-study work route, then a switch to sponsorship. In the UK this also exempts the employer from the Immigration Skills Charge, which makes you cheaper than an overseas hire.

**Build seniority at home first.** Three to five years of real responsibility moves you above the threshold and makes the public evidence stronger at the same time.

**Join a company that already has offices abroad**, and transfer internally once you have the qualifying service.

**Canada's Express Entry**, which has no salary floor and scores you on age and education — the one system where being young is an advantage rather than a disqualification.

## A note on titles and codes

One practical detail that costs developers offers: your job title and the occupation code your employer uses do not have to match, but your described duties and the code do.

"Full stack developer", "software engineer", "platform engineer" and "DevOps engineer" may all map to the same code, or to different codes with different going rates. The going rate decides your salary floor, so the code decides whether an offer is sponsorable.

Ask which code the employer intends to use in the first substantive conversation. If the answer is a code with a going rate above the offer, that is a problem to solve before anyone invests more time — and sometimes the fix is simply that a different, equally honest code fits the actual duties better.
