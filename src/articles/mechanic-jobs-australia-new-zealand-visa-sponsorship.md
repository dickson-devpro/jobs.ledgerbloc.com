## Can Mechanics Get Visa Sponsorship in Australia and New Zealand?

Yes — motor mechanics, diesel mechanics, motorcycle mechanics and automotive electricians are among the trade occupations that appear on Australia's skilled occupation lists and can be sponsored under the Temporary Skill Shortage (TSS) subclass 482 visa. New Zealand uses a separate route, the Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV), with its own accreditation and wage requirements. Both countries are experiencing genuine shortages in the trade, which means willing employers exist — but the process is detailed, sequential and employer-dependent. This guide works through every stage so you can assess whether you qualify and what you need to do in the right order.

---

## What the Job Actually Involves — and Why There Is a Shortage

### Day-to-day work in Australia and New Zealand

Mechanics in both countries work across passenger vehicles, light commercial fleets, heavy transport, agricultural machinery and motorcycles. The work is diagnosis-heavy: modern vehicles require fluency with scan tools, oscilloscopes and manufacturer-specific software (dealer technicians at franchise dealerships work in proprietary systems like Xentry for Mercedes or ISTA for BMW). Beyond diagnosis, the role covers scheduled servicing, brake and suspension work, clutch and gearbox replacement, air-conditioning systems, electrical fault-finding and increasingly hybrid and electric vehicle high-voltage systems.

The working environment varies considerably. A metro dealership in Sydney or Auckland may be air-conditioned, brand-funded and process-driven, with flat-rate pay structures and KPI targets. A regional independent in Queensland or the South Island might mean longer hours, broader work (machinery, trucks and cars all in the same week) and a closer relationship with the owner. Remote mining-adjacent workshops — servicing haul trucks, drill rigs and light vehicles on site — sit at the top of the pay scale and the remoteness spectrum.

### Why employers struggle to fill these roles

Australia and New Zealand both have ageing trade workforces. Apprenticeship completions have not kept pace with fleet growth, electrification complexity, or attrition from retirement. The automotive industry in Australia employs around 70,000 mechanics according to sector data, and unfilled vacancies have persisted for several years. Regional and remote areas are hardest hit — there is simply no local candidate pool, and mechanics with families are reluctant to relocate domestically when metro wages are comparable.

The push toward electric and hybrid vehicles is simultaneously displacing some older technicians who have not retrained and creating demand for those who have. Employers sponsoring overseas mechanics typically do so because they have exhausted local advertising, cannot meet production demands and are willing to carry the cost and administrative burden of sponsorship to fill the gap.

## What It Pays

Salaries are quoted as annual base in Australia, before superannuation (currently 11.5%, rising incrementally — verify the current rate with the Australian Taxation Office). Superannuation is paid on top of base salary by the employer and is effectively additional remuneration, though it sits in a fund you cannot access until retirement age.

### Indicative salary ranges (AUD, 2025–26 market)

| Role | Entry / Regional | Experienced / Metro | Specialist / Remote |
|---|---|---|---|
| Motor Mechanic (General) | AUD 60,000–68,000 | AUD 70,000–85,000 | AUD 85,000–100,000+ |
| Diesel Motor Mechanic | AUD 65,000–75,000 | AUD 78,000–92,000 | AUD 95,000–120,000+ |
| Automotive Electrician | AUD 65,000–75,000 | AUD 78,000–90,000 | AUD 88,000–105,000+ |
| Motorcycle Mechanic | AUD 55,000–65,000 | AUD 65,000–78,000 | AUD 75,000–90,000 |

*These are indicative ranges drawn from advertised positions and industry sources. Actual offers vary by employer, enterprise agreement, state and individual negotiation. They should not be treated as guaranteed or typical.*

What pushes pay upward: diesel and heavy-vehicle work, EV/hybrid certification, remote or fly-in fly-out rosters, roles under an enterprise agreement in a large fleet operation, and specialist brand certification at a prestige dealership. What pulls it downward: entry-level positions without brand certification, smaller independent workshops, metro areas with a denser candidate pool.

The 482 visa requires that the salary paid to the sponsored worker meets the Temporary Skilled Migration Income Threshold (TSMIT), a floor set by the government and reviewed periodically. Verify the current TSMIT figure on the Department of Home Affairs website — if the role you are offered pays less than this threshold, sponsorship is not possible regardless of what the employer tells you.

## The Visa Route in Detail

### Australia: Subclass 482 Temporary Skill Shortage

The 482 visa has three streams. Motor mechanics typically fall under the **Medium-term stream**, which currently permits a stay of up to four years and provides a pathway to the employer-sponsored permanent residence visa (subclass 186 — Temporary Residence Transition stream) after three years with the same employer. Always confirm which stream applies to your specific ANZSCO code before proceeding.

**Step 1 — Employer becomes an approved sponsor.** The business lodges a sponsorship application with the Department of Home Affairs. They must demonstrate financial viability, a commitment to not discriminating in favour of overseas workers, and a record of training Australian workers. This step is the employer's responsibility and cost.

**Step 2 — Employer lodges a nomination.** The employer nominates the specific position, demonstrating that it is genuine, that it matches the nominated occupation, that the salary meets TSMIT, and that they have made genuine attempts to recruit locally first (Labour Market Testing, unless exempted by a trade agreement). The Skilling Australians Fund levy is paid at this stage by the employer — the amount depends on business turnover and visa duration.

**Step 3 — You lodge the visa application.** Once the nomination is approved (or sometimes in parallel, depending on processing queues), you lodge the subclass 482 application. You must provide: passport and identity documents, skills assessment outcome from TRA, evidence of English language ability (IELTS, PTE, OET or equivalent — unless exempt), health examination results, character documents (police clearances from every country you have lived in for 12 months or more in the past 10 years), and work experience evidence (payslips, employment contracts, referee letters on company letterhead).

### New Zealand: Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV)

New Zealand's AEWV requires employers to first become accredited with Immigration New Zealand, then conduct a Job Check (demonstrating the role could not be filled locally), and then support your Individual Visa Application. The role must pay at or above the median wage threshold current at the time of application — check Immigration New Zealand's website for the current figure. Skills assessments for trades are handled through the New Zealand Qualifications Authority (NZQA) or Workforce Development Councils, depending on the occupation. Processing times and requirements for the AEWV have changed several times since its 2022 introduction; always verify current rules directly with Immigration New Zealand.

## Qualifications, Licensing and Skills Assessment

### Trade Recognition Australia (TRA)

TRA is the assessing authority for automotive trades in Australia. They assess your overseas qualifications against the Australian Certificate III in Light Vehicle Mechanical Technology (or the equivalent diesel, motorcycle or automotive electrical Certificate III). The assessment has two components: a documentary stage (certificates, transcripts, employment references, payslips) and in many cases a practical component if documentation alone is insufficient.

**Documents TRA typically requires:**
- Proof of trade qualifications (certificates, apprenticeship completion)
- Employment references confirming the type, duration and scope of work performed
- Identity documents
- Translated copies of any documents not in English (by a NAATI-accredited translator)

Processing times for TRA assessments vary — allow at least three to six months and check TRA's published timeframes before submitting.

### State and territory vehicle repairer licences

In addition to the TRA assessment (which is for visa and immigration purposes), most Australian states and territories require a separate **motor vehicle repairer's licence** before you can legally carry out work on customer vehicles. This is a consumer-protection licence issued by the relevant state or territory fair-trading or licensing authority — for example, NSW Fair Trading in New South Wales, Consumer Affairs Victoria, or the Department of Energy, Mines, Industry Regulation and Safety in Western Australia. Requirements differ between jurisdictions: some recognise the TRA skills assessment directly, others require additional steps. Research the specific rules for the state your employer is located in.

### English language requirements

Most mechanics will need to demonstrate English ability unless they hold a passport from an exempt country (UK, USA, Canada, Ireland, New Zealand for Australian applicants). The minimum for a 482 visa is typically IELTS 5.0 overall with no band below 4.5, or an equivalent score in an accepted test. Some employers and some states have additional requirements for licensing purposes.

## Which Employers Actually Sponsor Mechanics

Not every workshop can or will sponsor. Sponsorship involves significant time, legal cost and ongoing compliance obligations. The employers most likely to sponsor include:

**Franchise dealerships** — large dealer groups (Autosports Group, AP Eagers, Peter Warren, Eagers Automotive and similar) employ hundreds of mechanics across multiple sites and have HR infrastructure capable of managing visa compliance. They sponsor most frequently for brand-trained technicians where there is a specific skills gap.

**Fleet and logistics operators** — companies running large truck or van fleets (transport, mining logistics, utilities) need diesel mechanics continuously. Some have dedicated workshops and HR teams; smaller operators typically use a migration agent to manage the process.

**Mining and resources sector** — open-cut mining operations in Western Australia, Queensland and the Northern Territory maintain large on-site workshops. The pay is the highest in the sector, but the work is often FIFO (fly-in fly-out) with roster patterns such as two weeks on, one week off. These employers are well-practised at sponsorship because the local labour pool in remote areas is thin.

**Agricultural machinery dealerships** — John Deere, CNH Industrial, AGCO and their dealer networks are chronically short of trained ag-machinery technicians, especially in rural and regional areas.

**Independent workshops (less common)** — a smaller independent can become an approved sponsor, but many lack the administrative capacity. Those that do sponsor tend to be larger multi-bay operations or specialist workshops (prestige vehicles, performance, commercial vehicles).

To identify genuine sponsors, search Seek.com.au, Indeed Australia and Trade Me Jobs (New Zealand) filtering by "visa sponsorship" or "sponsorship considered". Cross-reference the employer against the Australian Business Register (ABN lookup) to confirm they are a real, registered business.

## Mistakes That Get Applications Rejected

1. **Submitting a TRA application with incomplete employment evidence.** TRA will reject or stall assessments without payslips, employer letters on letterhead confirming job title and duties, and full date ranges. Fix: gather complete employment documentation — including from former employers — before you apply, even if it takes months.

2. **Accepting a salary offer below TSMIT.** The nomination will be refused. Fix: before signing any offer letter, confirm the salary meets the current TSMIT threshold and ask the employer to confirm this in writing.

3. **Gaps in police clearances.** A common rejection trigger is failing to provide clearances for every country lived in for 12 months or more in the last 10 years. Fix: map every address you have lived at over the past decade and obtain the relevant clearances for each country.

4. **Health examination expiry.** Medical examinations for visa purposes have an expiry window. If the process takes longer than expected and your medical expires, it must be redone. Fix: time your medical examination to coincide with the later stages of the visa application rather than the beginning.

5. **Insufficient Labour Market Testing evidence.** If the employer has not properly advertised the role in the required timeframes and formats before lodging the nomination, it will be refused. Fix: ensure your employer (or their migration agent) runs LMT correctly — two advertisements over at least four weeks, in formats accepted by the Department of Home Affairs.

6. **Relying on a migration agent rather than a licensed one.** Receiving immigration advice from anyone other than a registered migration agent (RMA) listed on the MARA register, or a practising Australian lawyer, is illegal in Australia. Fix: verify any agent's RMA registration at mara.gov.au before engaging them.

7. **Applying for the wrong stream.** Choosing the short-term stream when your occupation is eligible for the medium-term stream loses you two years of stay and limits your PR pathway. Fix: confirm the stream with a registered migration agent and cross-check the occupation lists yourself.

8. **Inconsistencies between the TRA assessment and the visa application.** If the job title, duties or dates in your TRA application do not match your visa application documents, caseworkers flag it immediately. Fix: use identical descriptions and date ranges across every document.

## Realistic Timeline from Application to Arrival

The sequence below assumes no complications, a responsive employer and no document delays. In practice, delays at any stage compound.

| Stage | Who does it | Typical duration |
|---|---|---|
| Gather and translate documents, obtain police clearances | You | 4–8 weeks |
| Lodge TRA skills assessment | You | 3–6 months (check TRA's current published times) |
| Employer lodges sponsorship application | Employer | 4–12 weeks (government processing varies) |
| Employer lodges nomination | Employer | 4–10 weeks (after sponsorship approval) |
| You lodge 482 visa application | You | 4–12 weeks (government processing varies; check current times) |
| Medical examination and final checks | You | 1–2 weeks (arranged once requested) |
| Visa grant and travel arrangements | Both | 2–4 weeks |
| **Total — rough estimate** | | **8–16 months** |

Current government processing times are published on the Department of Home Affairs website and change frequently. Do not use estimates from forums or agents as a substitute for the official figures.

## Scam Warning: Protect Yourself

The mechanic visa-sponsorship space attracts fraudsters because the demand is genuine and candidates are highly motivated. Follow these rules without exception.

**No legitimate employer or agent charges you for a job offer.** Any person or organisation asking for payment in exchange for a job offer, a sponsorship letter, or an "expedited" visa is running a scam. Walk away immediately.

**Verify the employer is real.** Search the company name on the Australian Business Register (abr.business.gov.au) and check the ABN status. For New Zealand, use the Companies Register at companiesoffice.govt.nz. A legitimate employer will have a trading history, a real address and verifiable contact details.

**Verify any migration agent.** In Australia, only Registered Migration Agents (RMA) or Australian legal practitioners may give immigration advice for a fee. Check the MARA register at mara.gov.au. In New Zealand, only Licensed Immigration Advisers (LIA) may advise for a fee — check the register at iaa.govt.nz.

**The visa is granted by the government, not by an agent.** No agent has the power to guarantee a visa outcome or to "approve" your application independently of the Department of Home Affairs or Immigration New Zealand. Any claim to the contrary is false.

**Be especially cautious of offers found on social media or messaging apps.** Legitimate mechanic vacancies in Australia and New Zealand are advertised on Seek, Indeed, Trade Me Jobs, Gumtree Jobs and company career pages — not via WhatsApp groups, Facebook messages from strangers, or Telegram channels promising "guaranteed sponsorship".

If you believe you have encountered a scam, report it to the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (Scamwatch) at scamwatch.gov.au, or in New Zealand to Consumer Protection at consumerprotection.govt.nz.
